AESHA: Accelerating Eigen-decomposition-based Sparse
Transformer with Hybrid RRAM-SRAM Architecture
Xuliang Yu1 , Tianwei Ni1 , Xinsong Sheng1 , Yun Pan1 , Lei He2 , and Liang Zhao1,∗
1 College of Information Science and Electronic Engineering, Zhejiang University

2 University of California, Los Angeles; ∗ Corresponding author email: lzhao2020@zju.edu.cn

Abstract
Compute-in-memory (CIM) architectures based on emerging nonvolatile memories (eNVM) are recognized as promising candidates
for the efficient computation of self-attention-based Transformer
models, which are bounded by memory-intensive dynamic computations involving large matrices. However, existing CIM-based
approaches mostly focused on the acceleration of vector-matrix multiplications (VMM) to obtain 𝑄, 𝐾 and 𝑉 matrices for direct or sparse
calculations of the vanilla Transformer. By storing large amounts of
intermediate results in eNVM, the endurance limit is ignored which
contradicts the reality of these technologies. In this work, a hybrid
RRAM-SRAM CIM architecture to accelerate Transformers, namely
AESHA, is proposed based on the eigen-decomposition of 𝑊𝑄 𝑊𝐾𝑇
and the systolic in-memory attention reconstruction. By utilizing
the features of symmetric/skew-symmetric real matrices, AESHA
translates dynamic attention computation to RRAM-based sparse
feature transformation and SRAM-based systolic attention reconstruction to fully exploit the advantages of both on the architectural
level. Experiments on a broad spectrum of benchmarks demonstrate
that AESHA delivers superior performance in terms of pipeline
optimization and energy efficiency. Specifically, AESHA achieves
2.59×, 2.69×, 2.62×, 2.58×, 3.08× and 7.84× maximum static RRAM
memory footprint reduction in BERT-Base, BERT-Large, BigBird,
Sanger, ViT-Base and ViT-Large over vanilla computation stack,
respectively. It eliminates all runtime write access to the RRAM
arrays to circumvent the endurance limit. Additionally, AESHA
demonstrates 3170.0×, 95.6×, 4.1×, 19.0×, and 19.2× speedup, and
336.0𝐾×, 12.6𝐾×, 7.3×, 23.1×, and 25.8× energy reduction over
CPU, GPU, ReBERT, ReTransformer, and CPSAA, respectively.

In order to improve the energy efficiency and inference speed

optimization techniques. One endeavor prioritizes reducing computational overhead and memory footprint through layer-level, headwise, or token-grained entry pruning based on softmax importance

DRAM, creating a bottleneck for on-chip query-key computations.
Recently, CIM architectures have emerged as a new research
direction to accelerate Transformer computations with higher energy efficiency. These architectures merge the computation and
model storage into the same CIM array, effectively reducing off-chip
memory access and associated energy consumption. A substantial
body of accelerators employ conventional memory implementations (such as SRAM and Flash) as well as eNVMs like RRAM,

18] explore eNVM-friendly data access through techniques like
matrix decomposition or fine-grained dataflow designs to accelerate the VMM primitive in attention computation. Other studies
[15, 19] focus on optimizing the maximum value search in softmax
by investigating logic-in-memory or content-addressable matching.
In addition to these approaches, some research [20–23] employs
heterogeneous RRAM-SRAM CIM designs to balance low-precision
prediction and exact computation workloads towards providing
low-power and efficient solutions.

Instead of solely optimizing the pipeline based on the matrix

major contributions of this work are summarized as follows:
• We propose a novel eigen-decomposition-based attention
computation pipeline, which equivalently transforms attention computation into the orthogonal feature transformation
plus the outer-product-based attention reconstruction to
optimize the dataflow’s compatibility with CIM.
• We propose an adaptive energy-distribution threshold-based
pruning strategy, which achieves feature space compression
with principal feature vector retention in head granularity,
thus effectively reducing the memory occupation of static
weights and intermediate activations.
• We introduce a heterogeneous CIM implementation featuring a hybrid dataflow for versatile computation tasks in the
eigen-decomposition-based attention paradigm. Specifically,
we utilize analog weight-stationary (WS) based RRAM-CIM
for feature transformations, and digital output-stationary
(OS) based SRAM-CIM for attention reconstruction.
• Extensive experiments and ablation studies on various transformer models consistently validate the advantages of our
proposed AESHA framework, with throughput and energy

2.2

Motivation

Fig. 1(b) outlines some notable pipeline optimization efforts in
existing RRAM-based attention accelerators (ReTransformer and
CPSAA). These efforts primarily focus on mitigating data computewrite-compute (CWC) dependencies or shortening the computation
sequence. Nevertheless, they still contain the dimension coupling
between feature weight matrices (e.g., 𝑊𝑄 and 𝑊𝐾 ), making it difficult to apply canonical pruning strategies of deep neural networks
to compress attention weights. Even if compression scenarios are
achieved by matching the dimensions of query and key weights, it
is still challenging to establish optimization objectives for adaptive
attention recovery because the associated low-rank approximation
in attention maps may significantly lower the accuracy.
Furthermore, existing RRAM-based attention computation stacks
overlook the limitation of its endurance. In these studies, the dynamic input embeddings or intermediate activation results are frequently written into the RRAM arrays, which are used as buffer
memory. However, the endurance of commercial RRAM technologies is capped at approximately 106 cycles according to multiple
sources [26, 27], which contradicts with the high writing demands
of buffer memory. Minimizing the writing of intermediate results

Evaluation Methodology

We evaluate AESHA with benchmarks of two modalities using
encoder-based transformer models. For BERT [1] and its sparse
variants (BigBird [33], Sanger [14]), we test 8 language understanding tasks (MNLI, QNLI, QQP, SST2, STSB, RTE, COLA and MRPC)
from the GLUE [34] datasets with the default sequence length of 128.
For Vision Transformers (ViTs), we measure image recognition performance on 3 datasets (CIFAR10/100 and ImageNet1K [35]) where
a CNN with the kernel and step sizes of 16 is used as a backbone to
extract the features of 196 patches from 224 × 224 images.
We fine-tune pre-trained models from the Huggingface transformer library [36] when applying the pruning strategy to compress
attention feature spaces with 𝜏𝑢𝑛𝑖𝑡 = 0.1. All tasks are executed
using PyTorch v2.0 and CuDNN v11.8 on two Nvidia RTX 4090
GPUs. We adopt the fine-tuning parameters outlined in Sanger [14]
for BERT and its variants. For ViTs, we employ the Adam optimizer
with a learning rate of 3e-2 and a batch size of 128. The fine-tuning
epochs range from 5 to 15 depending on the downstream task.
We simulate the RRAM-CIM and SRAM-CIM macros using the
peripheral setups under 32nm node technology in NeuroSim [37].
The read and write pulse times of the RRAM cells are taken from
[38], whereas the on/off resistances of RRAM cells with the 4-bit
cell precision [39] are extracted from [26]. We verify the SA-based
LPU via Cadence Virtuoso and estimate the power of the OS-based
attention reconstruction engine and other digital modules using
Synopsys Design Compiler at 28nm technology. Additionally, we
measure Nvidia GPU and Intel CPU’s power by Nvidia-smi and
PyRAPL, and latency using CUDA Event and Python timer.

1) Impact of Pruning Threshold. We first analyze how pruning
thresholds affect model performance. As depicted in Fig. 7(a), the

Reducing the pruning threshold causes eigenspace compression.
Thus, the reconstructed attention matrix can not fully reflect the

Conversely, BERT-Large and ViTs demonstrate greater resilience

2) Impact of Pipeline Designs. We evaluate the speedup and
energy efficiency of four AESHA-based pipeline-optimized architectures (i.e., AESHA-WS, AESHA-OS, AESHA-PW and AESHA-PO)
and prior arts over the CPU baseline for self-attention processing of
BERTs and ViTs. The detailed definition of these AESHA variants
is given in the caption of Fig. 9. For optimized pipelines without
adopting the pruning strategy, AESHA-WS and AESHA-OS can
achieve higher speedups than ReBERT on BERT/ViT-Base models.
In contrast, speedups are comparable in BERT/ViT-Large counterparts. Compared to the general purpose computing platforms CPU,
GPU and CIM-based ReBERT, ReTransformer and CPSAA, AESHAPO achieves maximum inference speedups of 3170.0×, 95.6×, 4.1×,
19.0×, 19.2× on a single attention layer. In the BERT/ViT-Large models, AESHA-PO exhibits stronger speedup gains than AESHA-PW.
Two main reasons contribute to this phenomenon. First, adopting
the energy-distribution-based pruning strategy on deeper models
can achieve higher compression ratios and parameter reduction.
Second, AESHA-PO consumes fewer data movements and computation cycles than the compute-write-compute manner in AESHA-PW,
particularly in cases of low feature embeddings.
AESHA-PW and AESHA-PO both save more energy across models and benchmark datasets due to the pruned operations. Compared
to CPU, GPU, ReBERT, ReTransformer and CPSAA, AESHA-PO reduces energy consumption on the attention layers by 336.0𝐾×,
12.6𝐾×, 7.3×, 23.1×, 25.8×. AESHA-PO achieves energy reductions of 8.74% ∼ 9.36% more than AESHA-PW on BERT-Base and
22.18% ∼ 23.45% more reduction on BERT-Large. However, AESHAPO exhibits less energy-saving performance on ViTs compared to
AESHA-PW, since the OS-based dynamic attention reconstruction

engine consumes quadratically more energy with token numbers.
Notably, ViTs require longer input sequences than BERTs.
3) End-to-end Comparison. For a fair comparison, we evaluate
the throughput and energy efficiency of Transformers across different pipeline optimization approaches with the same hardware
configurations. As shown in Tab. 3, AESHA-PO yields the best overall throughput and energy efficiency. Compared to state-of-the-art
methods, AESHA-PO achieves 176 GOPS and 149 GOPS throughput on ViT/BERT-Base and ViT/BERT-Large models, indicating a
throughput improvement of 16.33% ∼ 31.24% from ReBERT. Additionally, AESHA-PO demonstrates 3.72×, 21.53×, 22.72× and 1.97×
energy efficiency improvements compared to ReBERT, ReTransformer, CPSAA and AESHA-PW. The energy efficiency values can
be further enhanced if multi-bit input is allowed for RRAM-CIM.

6

Conclusion

In this work, we present AESHA, an RRAM and SRAM-based algorithm and architecture co-design framework to accelerate the selfattention-based Transformer models efficiently. On the algorithm
level, we propose an eigen-decomposition-based attention computation pipeline and an energy-distribution-based structured pruning
strategy in order to enhance attention computation efficiency while
minimizing the memory footprint and RRAM write counts. On
the architecture level, we introduce a heterogeneous CIM implementation featuring hybrid dataflow to balance the workload of
static feature transformation and dynamic attention reconstruction.
Furthermore, we design a fine-grained systolic array to improve the
throughput and energy efficiency. Our AESHA achieves a compression ratio of attention weight parameters of 2.5× ∼ 2.69× on BERTs
and 3.07× ∼ 7.84× on ViTs with minimal performance degradation.
In addition, our AESHA achieves superior latency and power efficiency compared to prior arts, as evidenced by achieving 31.24%
higher throughput and 3.72× better energy efficiency than ReBERT.
AESHA presents a promising solution for lightweight, low-power,
high-throughput edge transformer inference applications.
