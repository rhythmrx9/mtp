As mentioned in Section 1, single bit RRAM devices have higher

Architecture Details

The overall architecture details are shown in Figure 2. From
the lower level to the higher level, the architecture is composed

and instruction executions are controlled by a Global Controller.

PE Slice. Each PE Slice consists of an Input Distributor, eight PEs
and Barrel Shifters, and a Shift-And-Add Unit. The Input Distributor
fetches the input feature data from the Shared Bus, splits the data
into multiple m-bit data, and loads them to eight PEs in each cycle.
The eight PEs in one PE Slice store at most eight bits of one weight

accuracy loss [20]. As shown in Equation (7), the results of each PE

shift operation with various shift base. After summing the shifted

used for calculating the �nal results.
PE Bank. When the length of the unrolled weight is larger than

split for mapping onto di�erent crossbars [16]. In our architecture
design, since one PE Slice is equivalent to a crossbar based on 8-bit
RRAM cells, we need to utilize the results of multiple PE Slices for
mapping the long kernel. Thus, adequate PE Slices are placed in
one RRAM Bank (i.e., 16 256 ⇥ 256 crossbars), to reduce the costs

Other Storage and Processing Units. When the computations

HARDWARE CONFIGURATION ANALYSIS

In the proposed architecture, di�erent con�gurations of the PE
will directly in�uence the computing accuracy and the hardware
overhead. The main design parameters of PEs include the design
choices of the interfaces and the crossbar size. This section will introduce the trade-o� between accuracy and hardware performance
considering di�erent design parameters.
Impact of DACs. The precision of DACs determines the maximum bit width of the input voltages of crossbars. On one hand,
when DACs precision is lower than the activation precision, multiple cycles are needed for loading data, which increases the latency.
However, most of the activation precision is reduced by 50% using
our proposed quantization method, high precision DACs are wasteful. On the other hand, Saberi et al. [14] demonstrated that the area
and power of DACs grow exponentially with the increasing precision; therefore, a high precision DAC must be shared by multiple
WLs, and we still need multiple cycles for loading activation to
di�erent WLs.
Impact of crossbar size. According to Equation (3), the crossbar
size in�uences the ideal precision of the BL current, and a�ects
the RRAM computing error further. Besides, since the crossbar size
determines the number of crossbars we need for storing the weights,
it will also a�ect the hardware area and energy overhead [24].
Combining the above considerations, theoretically, crossbars with
the larger size have the advantage of lower energy consumption,
