Abstract-With the superior algorithmic performances, BERT
has become the de-facto standard model for various NLP tasks.
Accordingly, multiple BERT models have been adopted on a
single system, which is also called multi-task BERT. Although the
ReRAM-based accelerator shows the sufficient potential to execute a single BERT model by adopting in-memory computation,
processing multi-task BERT on the ReRAM-based accelerator
extremely increases the overall area due to multiple fine-tuned
models. In this paper, we propose a framework for area-efficient
multi-task BERT execution on the ReRAM-based accelerator.
Firstly, we decompose the fine-tuned model of each task by
utilizing the base-model. After that, we propose a two-stage
weight compressor, which shrinks the decomposed models by
analyzing the properties of the ReRAM-based accelerator. We
also present a profiler to generate hyper-parameters for the proposed compressor. By sharing the base-model and compressing
the decomposed models, the proposed framework successfully
reduces the total area of the ReRAM-based accelerator without
an additional training procedure. It achieves a 0.26 x area than
baseline while maintaining the algorithmic performances.
I. INTRODUCTION

The transformer-based language models [4], [12], [19]
have become the de-facto standard model for various natural
language processing (NLP) applications [2], [10], [15], [21].
Also, BERT, which brings the NLP's ImageNet moment, is
the basis of the transformer-based language model. The unique
training procedure of BERT, which consists of pre-training
and fine-tuning, helps BERT understand the natural language
well and outperform the conventional algorithms (e.g., Seq2Seq
[23]) on various NLP tasks. Despite its remarkable algorithmic
performances, the larger model size and the lower operational
intensity (ops/weight byte) of BERT cause the memory wall
problem [26], where data traffic becomes the dominant portion
of energy consumption and latency [14], [17], [22].
The ReRAM-based accelerators [3], [20], [27] are proposed
to solve the data communication issue by adopting in-memory
computation. They utilize the intrinsic property of ReRAM,
where cell current is proportional to the applied voltage and
cell conductance. Whole weight parameters are stored across
ReRAM arrays in advance for the energy-efficient computation,
and the computation is performed by applying input data
to corresponding ReRAM arrays. By exploiting in-memory
computation, the ReRAM-based accelerator enables energyefficient execution of a single BERT model [29].
Despite its potential to process the single BERT model,
executing multi-task BERT on the ReRAM-based accelerator
induces a severe issue that exceedingly increases the overall

area. Multi-task BERT is defined as that multiple BERT
models for various NLP tasks (e.g., similarity checker, review
classification) are processed on a single system. The training
procedure of each multi-task BERT model consists of pretraining and fine-tuning stages. The fine-tuning process adjusts
the base-model, which is generated in the pre-training stage,
and it produces a corresponding fine-tuned model for each NLP
task. Accordingly, as the number of NLP tasks to be supported
in a single system increases, the number of fine-tuned models
also increases in multi-task BERT. However, since the whole
weight parameters should be programmed on ReRAM arrays
in advance, a larger number of fine-tuned models exceedingly
increase the total area on the ReRAM-based accelerator, which
also decreases the utilization of computing resources.
Several works [5], [7], [11], [22], [30] have been proposed in
the algorithm venue to solve the model size issue of multi-task
BERT. However, as they do not consider the properties of the
ReRAM-based accelerator, the methodologies in the previous
works have limitations on the ReRAM-based accelerator. Firstly,
the methodology in [30] requires fine-grained controls so
it cannot be supported by the ReRAM-based accelerator,
where the bulky MAC operation is performed. Also, although
other methodologies [7], [11], [22], [30] can be adopted on
the ReRAM-based accelerator, [11], [30] do not reduce the
total area, or [7], [22] even increase the computation latency.
Furthermore, all methods require an additional training process,
which could hurt other training-based BERT optimizations [6],
[18], [28]. Therefore, the proper solution to resolve the model
size issue is mandatory for the ReRAM-based accelerator.
In this work, we propose a framework for area-efficient
multi-task BERT execution on the ReRAM-based accelerator.
We aim to achieve high area efficiency on the ReRAM-based
accelerator without an additional training process. We observe
that the fine-tuned model of each task is highly correlated to
the base-model. Based on the observation, we first decompose
the fine-tuned model by utilizing the base-model. After that, we
propose a two-stage model compressor, which minimizes the
size of the decomposed model by considering the characteristics
of the ReRAM-based accelerator. We also present a profiler to
generate hyper-parameters for the two-stage compressor. In the
inference phase, the decomposed model and the base-model are
merged together on the ReRAM-based accelerator. By sharing
the base-model across multiple tasks and reducing the size of
the decomposed models, the area-efficient multi-task BERT
execution is realized on the ReRAM-based accelerator.

The major contributions of this paper are as follows.
• We analyze the characteristics of multi-task BERT.
• We propose a framework to reduce the size of multi-task

II. BACKGROUND AND MOTIVATION

A. Multi-task BERT

With the superior algorithmic performances, the transformerbased language models (e.g., BERT [4], RoBERTa [12], and
DistilBERT [19]) have become the de-facto standard model for
NLP applications. Among various transformer-based language
models, BERT [4], which outperforms the human ability for
the first time, is the basis of other models. Fig. 1 (a) describes
the model architecture of BERT. Firstly, the input embedding
layer converts input sequence words into corresponding word
vectors, and they are passed to cascaded encoders. Each encoder
includes self-attention block and feed-forward block. The selfattention block consists of fully-connected layers and attention
computation, while the feed-forward block contains only fullyconnected layers. The layer type forming each encoder is a
fully-connected layer except the attention computation.
As BERT outperforms the conventional algorithms on various
NLP tasks, multiple BERT models for various NLP tasks
(e.g., similarity checker, review classification) are adopted on
a single system, which is known as multi-task BERT. The
training of multi-task BERT consists of two stages: the pretraining and the fine-tuning for each task, which are shown
in Fig. 1 (b). Firstly, the pre-training process trains the model
to understand the natural language well. It generates the basemodel by utilizing the large-scale unlabelled dataset. As the
base-model comprehends general rules of the natural language,
it can be widely utilized for various NLP tasks. The fine-tuning
for each task is performed by utilizing the base-model. It tunes
the base-model according to the task with a small-scale taskspecific dataset. As a result, the fine-tuned model of each task
is produced.

B. ReRAM-based DNN accelerator

The ReRAM-based DNN accelerators [3], [20], [27] have
been proposed to execute DNN models efficiently by adopting
in-memory computation. Fig. 2 shows the general architecture
of the ReRAM-based accelerator. IMA is a unit processing
element (PE), which contains ReRAM computing arrays. A
weight value is represented by cells of positive and negative
arrays (WposIWneg). According to the sign value and the cell
resolution, the magnitude of weight value is stored in multiple
cells of the corresponding array. Also, weight parameters
of each output channel are allocated on the same bit-line.
Whole weight parameters are programmed on ReRAM arrays
in advance for the energy-efficient computation [3], [20], [27].
In the inference phase, the array-level MAC operation is
executed by exploiting the intrinsic property of ReRAM, where
the cell current is proportional to the applied voltage and
the cell conductance. Firstly, input data are converted into
analog voltage values via DAC and applied to the ReRAM
array through word-lines. All cells in the array generate the
cell current simultaneously, and each cell current represents a
multiplication result of the input and weight data. The various
cell current values are accumulated through a bit-line, and
the analog subtraction between results of the positive and
negative array is performed to generate partial sum results. The
results are converted to a digital value via ADC. S&A block
reconstructs a MAC operation output, and other non-linear
functions are executed by a non-linear unit (NU) in each tile.
C. Motivation

Although the ReRAM-based accelerator enables an energyefficient execution of a single BERT model [29], processing
multi-task BERT models on the ReRAM-based accelerator
suffers from the severe issue that extremely increases the overall
area. As described in Section II-A, the training process of
multi-task BERT contains the fine-tuning process for each task,
which produces the corresponding fine-tuned model. Thus, as
a single system supports a larger number of NLP tasks, the
system should hold a larger number of fine-tuned models [7],
[11], [30]. A larger number of fine-tuned models exceedingly
increase the total area on the ReRAM-based accelerator since
whole BERT models should be programmed on ReRAM arrays
in advance as described in Section II-B.

To handle the model size issue, several works [5], [7], [11],
[22], [30] have been presented for GPu. However, as previous
works are based on GPU, the methodologies have limitations
on the ReRAM-based accelerator. Firstly, [30] proposes the
masking method, which requires fine-grained MAC operations.
However, as the ReRAM-based accelerator performs the bulky
MAC operation in the array-level, it cannot be supported
by the ReRAM-based accelerator. Secondly, [5] generates
irregular zero values in weight parameters, and [11] requires
multiple BERT models. Thus, they do not reduce the total area
on the ReRAM-based accelerator. The methods in [7], [22]
even increase the computation latency on the ReRAM-based
accelerator since they increase the number of fully-connected
layers. Furthermore, all methodologies require an additional
training process. However, the training procedure is expensive
and it could hurt other training-based BERT optimizations
(e.g., token-pruning [6], layer-dropping [18], early-exit [28]).
Therefore, an appropriate solution to solve the model size issue
should be proposed for the ReRAM-based accelerator without
the additional training process.
III. PROPOSED FRAMEWORK

We propose a framework that enables an area-efficient multitask BERT execution on the ReRAM-based accelerator. The
framework generates the compressed weight allocation of multitask BERT by considering the characteristics of ReRAM-based
accelerator. In the inference phase, each NLP task is performed
with the compressed weight allocation. The observation and
detailed descriptions of the framework are as follows.
A. Observation

utilizing the base-model and small-scale task-specific dataset.
Accordingly, when fine-tuned(t) is defined as the fine-tuned

between the base-model and fine-tuned(t) by calculating cosine

and fine-tuned(t) are strongly correlated. When we define taskspecific(t) by subtracting the base-model from fine-tuned(t) as
shown in Fig. 3, task-specific(t) shows following aspects.
Since fine-tuned(t) is highly correlated to the basemodel, task-specific(t), which holds the difference between finetuned(t) and the base-model, has elements with small magnitude.

B. Two-stage model compressor

From the observations, we propose a two-stage model
compressor, which generates compressed weight allocation (Fig.
7 (b» of multi-task BERT without an additional training process.
Fig. 6 describes an overall process of the two-stage compressor.
We firstly decompose fine-tuned(t) as the combination of basemodel and task-specific(t). After that, we reduce the bit-width
and dimension of task-specific(t) by considering the properties
of both ReRAM-based accelerator and multi-task BERT. When
we compress task-specific(t), we utilize the cosine similarity as
the major metric since the cosine similarity considers non-zero
elements only, and it is independent of the vector length.

cells, which results in high area-efficiency on the ReRAMbased accelerator. Also, task-specific(t) has elements with small

Fig. 8 describes an overall procedure of the near-ternary
quantization process. It takes task-specific(t), ReRAM cell
resolution and max_prec(t), which is produced from the profiler,
as inputs. ReRAM cell resolution becomes the unit quantization
precision (e.g., I-I2-bit) in the near ternary quantization
since the number of ReRAM cells for weight parameters are
determined by the cell resolution. Also, max_prec(t) indicates
an upper bound of quantization for each NLP task. The overall
process is performed from the first layer to the last layer serially.
Firstly, weight parameters of task-specific(t) are quantized
to max_prec(t) by the clipping method (quantization phase
of iter 1 in Fig. 8 (b», which makes the resolution of the
quantized model the same as the original model. The elements
having a larger magnitude than the maximum value of given
precision are clipped to the maximum value. For example, the
element "4" is clipped to "3" in the 2-bit precision, while other
elements with "2" or "1" maintain the values as shown in Fig.
8 (a). Since the average magnitude of task-specific(t) is very
tiny, the quantization by the clipping method can maintain the
information of task-specific(t).
After the quantization phase, the assessment phase in Fig. 8
(a) is processed by utilizing the cosine similarity. If the cosine
similarity between the quantized task-specific(t) and initial taskspecific(t) is larger than 0.95, the assessment phase regards
that the quantized model has the potential to be quantized to
lower precision. Accordingly, the process performs the next

iteration with decreasing quantization precision by the unit
precision. The overall process is terminated when the cosine
similarity is smaller than 0.95 as shown in Fig. 8 (b). As a
result, task-specific(t)' with smaller bit-width is generated.
2) Selective hard-sharing: The ReRAM-based accelerator
performs the array-level computation, where all cells in an
array produce cell currents simultaneously. Also, various current
values in a bit-line are accumulated as described in Section
II-B. Therefore, the value-level control is not feasible in the
ReRAM-based accelerator, and eliminating the weight value
without considering the structure of the ReRAM array does not
enhance the area efficiency. Instead, removing output channels
reduces the total area on the ReRAM-based accelerator since
weight parameters of the same output channel are stored in
the same bit-line as described in Section II-B. Therefore,
we aim to eliminate output channels of task-specific(t) for
further compression. Eliminating the output channel of taskspecific(t) does not imply that fine-tuned(t) has zero elements
for the removed output channels. Instead, fine-tuned(t) shares
parameters of the base-model for the removed output channels
(hard-sharing). Since fine-tuned(t) is highly correlated to the
base-model as mentioned in Section III-A 0, hard-sharing
the base-model for several output channels does not hurt the
algorithmic performance.

E. Hardware support

As described in Section III-D, the base-model and the set
of compressed task-specific(t) are programmed on ReRAM
arrays. Since fine-tuned(t) is decomposed by the combination of
base-model and task-specific(t), the ReRAM-based accelerator
should add computation results of the base-model and taskspecific(t) to reconstruct the final output. Simultaneously, the
accelerator should consider the removed output channels of
compressed task-specific(t). We modify the digital logic of
the conventional ReRAM-based accelerator to implement the

1) ReRAM-based accelerator: We develop a cycle-level
simulator of the ReRAM-based accelerator to evaluate our
framework. The simulator is based on ISAAC [20] configuration with 32nm process node, and it utilizes positive
and negative arrays to store weight parameters. The detailed
configurations are as follows. The ReRAM array size is set
to 128 x 128, and the cell resolution is I-bit/cell. ADC/DAC
resolutions are set to 8-/1- bit, respectively. The precision of
both input and weight is 8-bit for the evaluation. We synthesize
additional digital logic for the proposed framework using
Synopsys Design Compiler with CMOS 65nm technology.
We estimate the area and energy overhead of the additional
hardware, and the logic is scaled to 32nm by following [8].
2) Workloads: We evaluate the proposed framework using

dataset [24] (CoLA, SST-2, MRPC, STS-B, QQP, MNLI,

additional training procedure, the fine-tuned model of each task

C. Algorithmic performance

Fig. 13 shows algorithmic performances of various design
configurations. We utilize BERT-base as a baseline. Firstly,
although the near-ternary quantization reduces the bit-width of
task-specific(t) into 1-12- bit, the accuracy degradation does
not occur in MTNQ . In other words, the combination of highprecision base-model and low-precision task-specific(t) can
provide identical algorithmic performances as the baseline.
Also, the selective hard-sharing successfully reduces the
dimension of task-specific(t) by considering the error resiliency
of each NLP task. Both error-vulnerable tasks (CoLA, RTE) and
error-resilient tasks (SST-2, WNLI) maintain the algorithmic
performances in MTacc with different selectivejactor(t). As
the user-defined mode allows accuracy degradation to further
reduce the total area, MTopt and MTagg , which produce more
compressed task-specific(t) than MTacc , provide 0.7 and 2.7
average score degradation from baseline, respectively. In conclusion, the proposed framework, which highly compresses taskspecific(t), successfully maintains the algorithmic performance
of each NLP task without the additional training procedure.

each number of tasks. Firstly, since the proposed framework
stores the base-model and task-specific(t) separately, it requires
a larger area than baseline when the number of tasks is 1.
Nevertheless, as the number of tasks increases, the proposed
framework effectively reduces the total area from baseline by
sharing the base-model across multiple tasks and compressing
the set of task-specific(t). In detail, when the task number is 2,

baseline, respectively. MTopt and MTagg reduce maximally
77% and 79% of total area. Furthermore, as the number of
tasks increases, the portion of base-model in the proposed
framework decreases. The base-model in MTacc occupies 80%
of the total area in the two tasks system while it accounts for
about 50% in the eight tasks system. To conclude, the proposed
work successfully reduces the total area of multi-task BERT
on the ReRAM-based accelerator. Also, the area reductions
increase in the system with a larger number of tasks.

E. Further analysis

1) Cell resolution: Fig. 16 (a) describes evaluation results of
the proposed framework on various cell resolutions. The nearternary quantization process utilizes the cell resolution as the
unit precision, and the average magnitude of task-specific(t)
is about one for all NLP tasks. Therefore, a single cell is
sufficient to express an element of task-specific(t) regardless of
cell resolutions. Therefore, the area reduction of the proposed
work is the largest in the low cell resolution, which is regarded
as the practical ReRAM cell resolution [13]. MTNQ on I-bit,
2-bit, and 4-bit resolutions achieve 0.26 x, 0.38 x, and 0.63 x
area than baseline, respectively. Accordingly, the area reduction
of I-bit MTacc is 1.4 x and 2.3 x larger than 2-bit and 4-bit
resolutions, respectively.

the proposed work on various base-models. We utilize BERTlarge [4], RoBERTa-base [12], and DistilBERT [19] for the
evaluation, and the results are normalized to baseline with each
base-model. Since all base-models adopt the two-stage training
procedure, the proposed framework successfully reduces the
total area on the various base-models. MTacc on BERT-large,
RoBERTa-base, and DistilBERT reduce 74%, 76%, and 63%
of total area from baseline, respectively. In conclusion, the
proposed work can be generally adopted to reduce the total
area for the various base-models.
Additionally, as each base-model shows different model
configurations (e.g., layer number, hidden size), the multi-task
system with each base-model shows a different total area. In
detail, MTacc on DistilBERT, which has the smallest basemodel size among various models, requires the smallest area
on the ReRAM-based accelerator as shown in Fig. 16 (c).

[5], [7], [11], [22], [30]. Firstly, the proposed framework does
not require the additional training procedure, which can hurt
the other training-based BERT optimizations [6], [18], [28].
However, all methodologies in [5], [7], [11], [22], [30] are based
on the training process. Secondly, the proposed work can reduce
the total area while maintaining the computation latency on the
ReRAM-based accelerator. However, the other methodologies
have limitations on the ReRAM -based accelerator since they do
not consider the characteristics of the ReRAM-based accelerator.
In detail, [5], [11], which produces irregular zero elements or
generates multiple fine-tuned models, do not reduce the area.
Also, since each fully-connected layer becomes a pipeline stage
with the same computation latency, [7], [22], which increase
the number of fully-connected layers, increase the computation
latency on the ReRAM-based accelerator. In conclusion, the
proposed methodology is the most suitable approach to handle
the model size issue on the ReRAM-based accelerator.
G. Overhead analysis

The overall process of the proposed framework is performed
once in the pre-processing phase. Therefore, it does not hurt
the inference performances, and the pre-processing time can
be amortized in many inferences. For the summation process
between the base-model and task-specific(t), we modify the
digital S&A block of the ReRAM-based accelerator. The
modified S&A block accounts for less than 0.1 % in total
energy consumption and area, and the MAC operation for taskspecific(t) is reduced from compressed task-specific(t). Two
aspects minimize the overhead of the summation process.
V. CONCLUSION

In this paper, we proposed the framework for the areaefficient multi-task BERT execution on the ReRAM-based
accelerator. We analyzed the characteristics of multi-task
BERT, and we decomposed the fine-tuned model of each
task by utilizing the base-model. After that, we proposed the
framework to compress the decomposed models by considering
the properties of the ReRAM-based accelerator. By sharing
one base-model and compressing decomposed models, the
proposed framework reduced maximally 74% of the total area
while maintaining the algorithmic performances.
