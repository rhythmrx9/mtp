1. From computing-in-memory to learning-in-memory
A crossbar array of analog programmable resistors (or memristive devices, or memristors), in principle, can
accelerate all the three elementary neural network operations: information forwarding, error
backpropagation, and weight update [1, 2]. By coordinating the applied read voltages and measurements of
the output currents, the vector-matrix multiplications for information forwarding and error
backpropagation can be done in one step [3, 4]. By properly applying write pulses on the crossbars, the
vector–vector outer production & accumulation can also be conducted parallelly in all synaptic devices [1].
The benefits of deep neural network acceleration with memristive crossbar arrays are threefold: (i) analog
computing is more efficient than digital computing; (ii) data transfer is minimized; (iii) the training and the
inference happen in the same hardware.
Unfortunately, most of the recent works focus on the demonstration of the neural network acceleration
for inference on edge applications which only consists of the information forwarding process [5–12]. The
acceleration of the error backpropagation has been proposed but has been seldomly demonstrated. The
in-situ weight update process was either performed in the offline training scheme [13–15] or performed one
by one and coordinated by external closed-loop control circuits [16, 17].
In inference-only neural networks (figure 1(a)), the neural network parameters are obtained elsewhere,
for instance, in a von Neumann architectured computing system. That is, the training or learning of the
neural network is performed in another computing system. This scheme is usually referred to as offline

During the inference stage, the memristive devices act as the memory for the weights and, simultaneously,
process the forwarding information. The neural network weights are kept in the non-volatile states of the
memristive devices; thus, no other volatile or non-volatile memory units are needed, nor the shuttling of

storage of the neural network parameters and the in-field neural network computation are superior for low
footprint and low energy consumption than the von Neumann architecture. Thus, it finds a niche for edge
computation. This strategy of using non-volatile memristive devices both for memory and neural processing
is commonly referred to as computing-in-memory (CiM). The CiM does not fully exploit the benefits of the
memristive crossbar array. Nevertheless, it paved the way for a more advanced learning-in-memory system.
In another aspect, the human brain not only computes in memory but also learns in memory (LiM). The
learning is conducted without the coordination or supervision of a von Neumann architecture system. Thus
it is of great interest to enable online training or online learning, which refers to the scheme that conducts all
three essential operations of a deep neural network in memristive crossbar arrays [18]. This means that there
will be no other separate computers of von Neumann architecture involved in the memristive learning
system (figure 1(b)). After the training procedure was finished, the same hardware could be employed to
conduct the inference task, where only signal forwarding operations are needed.
While there are hybrid training approaches between the offline training and the online training, they are
not the ultimate solution. During the hybrid training, the memristive array were used for the signal
forwarding, while the error backpropagation and weight gradient calculations were conducted on a
computer. The weight updates are performed in the memristive array after a single batch of training samples
is processed [19–21].
To enable the LiM schemes, efficient error backpropagation using the crossbar array of memristive
devices and appropriate weight updates methods are needed. Here, we review the difficulties in (i)
implementing error backpropagation (especially in convolutional layers) and in (ii) updating weights of
memristive crossbars with the existence of various non-idealities of synaptic behaviors. Various approaches
that have been proposed and should be further investigated to address these difficulties are discussed.
We think the summarization of these approaches and difficulties is a good reference for the
neuromorphic simulations work and eventually benefits the development of neuromorphic hardware using
crossbar arrays of memristors. We hope this work will bridge the gap between the device engineers who are
struggling to develop an ideal synaptic device and neural network algorithmists who are assuming that ideal
devices are right at hand. The close of this gap could push forward the information processing system
paradigm from CiM to LiM, aiming at a standalone non-von-Neumann computing system.

2. Elementary operations of deep learning
Deep learning, the powerful neural network algorithm for today’s artificial intelligence, consists of three
essential operations within each layer of the neural network: the information forwarding, the error
backpropagation, and the weight update, as illustrated in figure 2(a)–(c), respectively. These three operations
correspond to a vector-matrix multiplication, a reciprocal version of the vector-matrix multiplication, and a
vector–vector outer production & accumulation, respectively, resulting in huge demands in computational
resources in computing systems of von Neumann architecture. Here we use the fully connected layer to
illustrate the basic algorithm of neural network operations. More issues existing in the hardware
implementation of other typical layers, such as convolutional layers, will be covered in subsequent sections.

multiplying the input vector x with the weight matrix w (i.e. vector-matrix multiplication), and then
processing the production vector y = xw through an activation function σ (·) (figure 2(a)). The output of the
activation function, vector z = σ (y), is the input of the next layer.

2.4. The computational load of deep learning
Since all the element values in the neural network operations are usually represented by floating-point
numbers, completing the calculation denoted by equation (1) in a computer of von Neumann architecture,
needs to conduct the multiplications of two floating-point numbers for m × n times and additions (or
accumulation) of two floating-point numbers for approximate m × n times. Each pair of multiplication and
accumulation is called one multiply & accumulate operation (MAC). Thus, conducting the vector-matrix
multiplication needs to perform 2 × m × n floating-point operations (FLOPs) or m × n MACs. The error
backpropagation needs the same number of MACs for the vector-matrix multiplication (equation (4)).
Combining equations (5) and (6), we see that, for the weight update process, we need m × n times of
multiplications of three scalars (xi , δyj , and η) and m × n times of accumulations. In total, the single neural
network layer needs 3 × m × n MACs for training the neural network with one training sample.
The activation function also requires arithmetic calculations. The number of calculation steps depends
on the choice of the activation function. For instance, rectified linear unit (ReLU) requires fewer calculation
steps than sigmoid activation functions. Basically, the number of calculation steps is proportional to the
number of neurons as denoted by equations (2) and (3).
For a typical three-layer fully connected neural network with the size of 784–500-250-10 for the
recognition of handwritten digits in the modified National Institute of Technology (MNIST) dataset, the
number of weight elements or parameters is 519, 500 (784 × 500 + 500 × 250 + 250 × 10). Roughly,
approximate 1.5 million (519, 500 × 3) MACs need to be performed for training an image without counting
the calculations for the neural activations (equations (2) and (3)). The training set in the MNIST datasets
contains 60 000 images and successful training of the neural network usually takes about hundreds of epochs,
which needs totally 9 × 1012 MACs for 100 training epochs.
For a convolutional layer in deep neural networks, where weight connections are spatially shared, and for
a recurrent layer, where weight connections are temporally shared, the core computations are still the

In modern deep neural networks, the number of parameters and the required FLOPS are too large to be
handled efficiently. For instance, ResNet-50, a 50-layer residual network has 25.6 million parameters and
needs to perform 4.1 billion MACs for forwarding a single input image [22, 23]. Thus, training of a single
image roughly takes 12.3 billion MACs. The ResNet-50 was trained with 1.28 million images from the
ImageNet 2012 classification dataset and 128 epochs [22], counting up to 9.4 × 1018 MACs. AlpahGoZero
[24] and AlphaZero [25] developed by DeepMind increase the computation demands, i.e. the cost in dollars,
by roughly 3–4 orders of magnitude compared with ResNet-50 [26, 27].
These operations could be performed sequentially or parallelly in the floating-point units of a central
processing unit or a graphics processing unit. First, the complementary metal-oxide-semiconductor circuit
performing the high-precision MACs is complex and consumes large static and dynamic energy. Second, the
data of the forwarding information, the backpropagation errors, and the neural network parameters are
stored in off-chip memory. They are shuttled between the off-chip memory and the on-chip floating-point
units, which consumes much more energy than the MAC operations themselves and results in high latency.
This is known as the memory wall issue.
For the first issue, there are neural network quantization methods to reduce the precisions of the
forwarding information and the parameters, resulting in low-precision or integer-based neural networks [28,
29]. The second issue can be addressed by moving the off-chip memory close to the processing units and
reforming the data flow, as can be seen in multiple application-specific integrated circuits chips specified for
artificial intelligence, such as Google’s tensor processing unit [28], Cambrian’s DaDianNao [30], Microsoft’s
Azure Maia AI Accelerato3 , and Amazon’s Trainium24 .

The multiplication is conducted by Ohm’s law inherently, i.e. the current through the resistor is the
product of the applied voltage and the conductance of the resistor, while the accumulation or addition is
conducted by Kirchhoff ’s current law, i.e. the currents through the resistors in the same column are merged
in the bottom bar. Instead of performing the MACs in the vector-matrix multiplication one-by-one as
denoted by equation (1), all MACs are performing parallelly in one step governed by Ohm’s law and
Kirchhoff ’s current law as denoted by equation (11).
To complete the MAC operation by Ohm’s law and Kirchhoff ’s current law, accurate voltages
(equation (8)) need to be applied to the top bars of the array and the currents from the bottom bars
(equations (9) and (10)) need to be accurately sensed. The application of accurate voltages generally requires
digital-to-analog convertors (DACs). Accurate sensing of current generally requires analog-to-digital
convertors (ADCs) along with other auxiliaries, such as, transimpedance amplifier (TIAs) sampling and hold
circuits [31]. In this way, the MAC operations are conducted in analog domain, and the neuron
functionalities (such as activation function) are performed in digital domain. However, these peripheral
circuits complex the MAC operations and are costly in both area and energy consumption.
Other than conducting the MAC operation using voltage amplitude to represent the input value and
current amplitude to represent the output value, respectively, it is also possible to perform the MAC
operation in time domain [32, 33]. That is, the input value is represented by the pulse number or pulse
duration with fixed amplitude, and the output value is obtained by integrating the current from the bottom
bars. Still, complex peripheral circuits around the crossbar array are need.
Alternatively, the neuron functionalities can be performed in analog domain. For instance, Kiani et al
reports a fully hardware-based memristive multilayer neural network with the ReLU activation function is
implemented by analog circuits [34]. The current output of the preceding layer is converted to voltages and
rectified by a hybrid circuit of TIAs and rectifying diodes. This arrangement removes the needs of the
calculation of activation function in the digital domain which is usually conducted by an arithmetic logic
unit.
The accuracy of MAC operation is affected by several non-idealities. The inaccuracy of
weight-conductance mapping (equation (7)), when writing the weight to the conductance of memristive
devices. Read noise and conductance drift in long term could also degrade the accuracy of MAC calculation
[35, 36]. The truncation error of DACs and ADCs with limited bit width is another source of inaccuracy for
MAC operation [37]. However, the MAC calculation accuracy is not the only factor that affects the
performance of the neural network. For inference-only neuromorphic system, the degradation of the neural
network performance due to the inaccuracy of MAC operation can be compensated by hardware-aware
training [13]. For hybrid and online training, the neural network could usually be adapted to minimize the
effect of these non-idealities [19].
3.2. Information forwarding in convolutional layers
Information forwarding in a convolutional layer is implemented by unrolling the two-dimensional image
patches and weight kernels into single-column vectors [16, 38]. Different kernels are arranged together to
form a matrix which could be implemented by a memristive crossbar array.
Figure 3(a) illustrates the operation of a typical convolutional layer with 3 input feature maps, 2 output
feature maps, and 3-by-2 convolutional kernels. Each kernel has a size of 2-by-2. The first group of kernels
connects the three patches of input feature maps into one pixel of the first output feature map. The second
group of kernels connects the same three patches of input feature maps into one pixel of the second output
feature map.
Thus, the weights in the convolutional kernels can be mapped to a crossbar array of memristors, with the
size of 12-by-2 as shown in figure 3(b). Weights in the same kernel are unrolled to the same column. For
simplicity, the reference column for representing sign weights is depressed in the illustrative figure.

Input voltage vectors that are converted from image patches are fed to the crossbar array to generate

sequentially. This means that the spatially shared weight kernels are actually temporally shared for sliding
windows, which increases the latency of information forwarding in the convolutional layer. It is also possible
to duplicate the memristor array such that different input image patches can be convoluted parallelly. A
trade-off between memristor array size, i.e. the footprint of the memristive acceleration module, and the
latency should be made.

4. Error backpropagation through memristive crossbar array
4.1. Backward vector-matrix multiplication
For training a deep neural network on a conventional computer, the error backpropagation consumes the
same amount of computation resources as the signal forwarding. The implementation of error
backpropagation of a fully connected layer with a memristive crossbar array seems straightforward if only
looking at figures 2(b) and (e). However, several issues prevented the realization.
The implementation of the error backpropagation on the memristive crossbar array is similar to that of
the information forwarding, except that: (i) the vector-matrix multiplication direction is reversed, i.e. the
input voltage vector is applied to the bottom bars and the current output vector is measured in the top bars;
(ii) a row of reference resistors instead of a column of reference resistors is needed.
A duplicated set of voltage sources (e.g. DACs) and current meters (e.g. TIAs; sampling and hold circuits;
ADCs) is needed. It is also possible to reroute the voltage sources and current meters that are used for
forwarding VMM to conduct the task of backward VMM. In fact, in the forwarding VMM, the voltage
sources and current meters usually are not exclusively assigned to each row and column bar of the
memristive array. In most reported works, multiple row bars share one voltage source and multiple column
bars share one current meter, to reduce the overall footprint of the peripheral circuit. This arrangement,
however, reduces the throughput of the VMM operations. In addition, the error signals are small, which need
more accurate voltage sources tuning and currents sensing for the backward VMM.
4.2. Error backpropagation in a convolutional layer
The forwarding VMM and backward VMM in the same crossbar array in figures 2(b) and (e) are not valid in
convolutional layers, where spatially shared weights are needed. This is because the deconvolution operation
needs to reshape both the weight kernels and the convolutional patches.

between forward and backward passes of the convolutional layer operations. Alternatively, one can also use
separated crossbar arrays of memristors for the forward and backward paths. However, the conductance of
elementary memristors should be updated at the same pace.
4.3. Possible ways of by-passing the error backpropagation
The error backpropagation algorithm is a mathematically elegant learning algorithm, however, it is not
supported by physiological observations [39]. This is consistent with the situation in electrical circuit
hardware. In other words, it is hard to backpropagate the learning errors in the neuromorphic approach.
Novel learning methods that are more biologically plausible, and thus may also be more suitable for the
neuromorphic implementation, should be explored.
Autoencoder networks based on redistricted Boltzmann machines and deep belief networks [40, 41]
remove the need for error backpropagation for learning deep neural networks. Instead of error
backpropagation, contrastive divergence and greedy learning methods are used for single-layer learning and
multiple-layer learning, respectively [42].
There is also another more radically different and biologically plausible way to conduct the credit
assignment task for deep learning, named feedback alignment [43]. In the feedback alignment method, the
backpropagation of errors is not necessary through the symmetric connections of the forwarding path. The
implementation of this novel learning scheme is, however, an open issue to be explored by the research
community of neuromorphic computing.

However, the ideal memristive plasticity shown in figures 5(b) and (c), is not available in practical
electronic devices. Biological synapses do not show such ideal plasticity either. In other words, neither
memristive devices nor biological synapses can support the parallel weight update rules denoted by
equations (13) and (14) for deep learning. Thus, modification of the weight update rules should be made
both to enable the online deep learning of memristive deep neural networks and to shed light on possible
learning mechanisms of biological systems.
5.2. Plasticity of memristive devices
The non-idealities of the real memristive devices that prevent the application of the ideal weight update
scheme include non-linearity, write variations, limited conductance states, limited on/off ratio, and limited

retention time [46]. Among them, the non-linearity and write variations are the most troublesome issues.
Figures 5(d) and (e) show typical conductance changes under continuous identical pulses which aims to
increase and decrease the conductance of the memristive device, respectively [45]. These curves are often
referred to as long-term potentiation (LTP) and long-term depression (LTD) behaviors of the artificial
synapses, respectively.
The LTP and LTD behaviors of memristive devices are usually measured under continuous identical
pulses. This is because the conductance changes at various write pulse widths and pulse amplitudes can be
generally projected through the conductance tuning curves obtained from measured LTP and LTD behaviors
under identical pulses. On the other hand, however, tuning the conductance by identical pulses (fixed pulse
width and pulse amplitude for LTP and LTD) is friendly for hardware implementations of the in-situ weight
update during online learning.
From the actual LTP and LTD behaviors of memristive devices, we can see that the conductance of
memristive devices is bounded and tends to be saturated when the conductance is close to the conductance
limits at either tune direction. This is the natural behavior of any physical device since the internal changes of
the device that are induced by external stimuli will naturally decrease when the state of the device
approximates its physical bounds. The write variations, including cycle-to-cycle variations and
device-to-device variations, are also natural behaviors of memristive devices, originating from various
resistive switching mechanisms.
A behavior-level model of the conductance changes for the LTP and LTD is usually needed to investigate
the deep learning performance under various non-idealities of the memristive devices [46, 47]. The
behavior-level model needs to incorporate the non-linear weight update behavior, the limited on/off ratio,
limited conductance levels, the asymmetry between LTP and LTD, and write variations, as shown in
figure 5(f) [42]. The model is also necessary to validate the various weight update schemes to achieve the
LiM goal.
5.3. Weight update schemes
With the existence of the non-idealities of memristive synaptic devices, the parallel weight updating scheme
shown in figure 5(a) would deviate significantly from the ideal case. Thus, modified and mostly
compromised variants of weight update methods are applied. These compromised versions might (i) be only
possible to update synapse weights of a crossbar array of memristive device row-by-row or
column-by-column, or even single-device-by-single-device; (ii) need multiple write-and-read iterations to
perform one weight update operation of a single memristive device.

After all the weight change directions are obtained, the conductance of corresponding devices are updated

These ‘set’ style operations and ‘reset’ style operations can be performed column-by-column (or
row-by-row) in the memristive crossbar array. For instance, the memristive devices that needed to be
potentiated in the first column are parallelly potentiated in a half-write-voltage scheme, that is, half of the
write voltage on the column bar and half of the negative write voltage on the row bars of corresponding
devices, as shown in figure 6(a).

learning rule. First, the Manhattan rule itself disregards the magnitude of the gradient of the loss function to
the weights. Second, the conductance changes induced by the identical write operations on memristor
devices are not uniform as required by the ideal Manhattan learning rule. As a result, the learning ability of
the Manhattan learning rule is limited. On the positive side, the conductance of the memristive device is

Manhattan learning rule or the threshold Manhattan learning rule could be successfully applied to preceding
layers using the loss gradient calculated by feedback errors.
5.3.2. Occurrence of stochastic streams
To increase the learning accuracy, it is essential to keep the magnitude of the gradient loss. While identical
write pulses are strongly recommended considering the hardware implementation of the write or weight
update circuit, the magnitude of gradient loss can be encoded by the probability of the identical write
operations.
Figure 6(b) illustratively shows the design of using the probability of write operations to update a single
weight element [50]. The weight update is performed by applying stochastic pulse streams on the two sides
of the memristive devices. The probability of the negative half-voltage write pulse on one side is proportional
to the forwarding signal xi , while the probability of the half-write pulse on another side is proportional to the
feedback error δyj . The occurrence of the negative half-voltage write pulse and the half-write pulse, which
has the probability of xi δyj , results in the decrease of the conductance changes.
In this method, the pulse streams can be parallelly applied to all row bars and column bars of the
memristive array. In addition, the calculation of the gradient loss to each device has already been conducted
in situ, i.e. the production xi δyj do not need to be performed externally.
The non-uniform conductance change under identical write pulses would be a major problem of the
online training [50, 54]. Bounded conductance ranges, non-linear weight updates, and write variations are
the major sources of the degradation of the training accuracy. When all these non-idealities are well
suppressed by device engineering, which is not an easy task, near software training accuracy could be
obtained.
The Manhattan learning rule and occurrence of stochastic streams are both blind write methods, because
they conduct the write operations without caring about the current states of the memristive devices [55].
5.3.3. Read-write
If the weight update behaviors of the memristive devices, which depend on the current state and the strength
of excitation, can be well predicted, the non-linear weight update issue could be passed. In the read-write
method, for a single weight update, a read operation is needed to determine the current state of the

memristive devices. The device state is updated by applying a write operation whose strength is calculated
depending both on the current state of the device and the requested amount of the weight change [19–21].
For instance, when the conductance increase is calculated, the pulse width of potentiation pulses could be
adjusted according to the current conductance states and the LTP curve, as shown in figure 6(c). If the
current conductance is near the minimal conductance limit, a shorter potentiation pulse should be applied.
Otherwise, a longer potentiation pulse is needed. Similar operations are needed for the conductance
depression requirement by the learning algorithm.
This weight update method needs one read operation before the write operation to obtain the current
conductance of the memristor device and could not be parallelly conducted for all memristive devices in the
crossbar array. In addition, variable pulse width is not friendly for hardware implementation of the write
circuit.
5.3.4. Read-write-verify
The accuracy of conductance tuning of memristive devices could be greatly improved if the read-write-verify
method is employed, which suppresses the non-linear weight update and write variation effects [16, 17, 56].
This method is also called the closed-loop write method.
First, the current conductance of the memristive device is read out and the conductance change is
calculated from the learning algorithm. They determine a target conductance of a write and verify loop.
In the loop, if the current conductance is lower or higher than the target conductance, a potentiation or a
depression pulse is applied, respectively. Then the new current conductance state is read out to verify if it has
been written to a state within a range near the target conductance. Otherwise, the loop is continued. One can
use increasing pulse amplitude or uniform pulse amplitude when continuous potentiation or depression
pulses are needed in the loop [57].
The closed loop read-write-verify method is an effective, but not an efficient, way to fine-tune the
conductance of memristive devices in a crossbar array. The accuracy of the conductance tuning is defined by
defined resolution near the target conductance. The higher the resolution, the longer the write and verify
loop. The longer write and verify loop, however, would greatly slow the online learning process, since
hundreds of write operations are needed in a single loop and it could only be performed device-by-device in
the crossbar array. Yao et al have demonstrated that this read-write-verify method could tune the
conductance of a memristive device to have a 5-bit representation ability (32 levels) [16]. Higher write
accuracy could be obtained in principle if a longer write and verify loop is tolerable by the learning process.
5.3.5. Periodical carry
The periodical carry algorithm uses more than one artificial synapse for each weight cell of the weight array.
The gradient losses are used to update the low significant weights, and the higher significant weights are
periodically updated when detected that the lower significant weights are out of their limits and generate
carry signals. Different high-significant and low-significant weight implementation methods have been
proposed. Agarwal et al proposed to use memristive devices for both high and low significant weights, as
shown in figure 6(d) [44]. Ambrogio et al proposed to use memristive devices as the high significant weights
and capacitor-based artificial synapses as the low significant weights [58–60].
A similar approach, named as ‘tiki-taka’ algorithm [61], has also been proposed. Instead of updating the
high significant weights after the low significant weights overflow or reach a threshold, the ‘tiki-taka’
algorithm updates the high significant weights according to a randomly selected small patch of the low
significant weights. The high-significant weights have a much lower update frequency than the
low-significant weights, which is similar to the periodical carry method.
The periodical carry method is effective in mitigating the troubles induced by the non-idealities of
conductance updates. It enables the possibility of parallel weight updates for all synapses in the crossbar
array, thus promising efficient online in-memory deep learning.
It is further found that the low significant weights are not necessary to participate in the information
forwarding and error backpropagation processes of the neural network [51, 62–64]. Thus, the gradient of the
loss to the weight could be accumulated in a separate array outside of the crossbar of memristive devices, as
shown in figure 6(f). The separated array could be a high-precision digital memory [51] or digital counters
[42] according to the different ways of calculating the gradient of loss to the weight. The separation of the
accumulation of the gradient loss and the conductance updates is highly tolerant to various non-idealities of
memristive synaptic behaviors with the blind write method and greatly reduces the number of write
operations on memristive devices.

6. Discussion and conclusion
To enable in situ deep learning in analog memory, innovations from both learning algorithms and synaptic
devices are needed.
From the aspect of learning algorithms, the conventional error backpropagation method is not friendly
for a crossbar array of memristive devices, especially for layers that are not fully connected, such as
convolutional layers. This is consistent with the case in biological LiM systems, where error backpropagation
through the same synaptic path in a reversed direction is not supported [39]. Continuous learning algorithm
innovations in network topology are intensively needed.
From the side of the synaptic devices, materials and devices engineering toward ideal synaptic behaviors
are always required. However, for even quite noisy and nonlinear synaptic behaviors, there are also ways to
compensate them using different weight update schemes at various costs, in terms of the complexities and
area of the write circuits, the number of write operations, and the energy and the latency of the weight
update. Device engineers should avoid claiming high in situ deep learning performance without mentioning
the exact methods for error backpropagation and weight updates.
Nonlinearity and write variations are the most heinous properties from the point of view of learning
algorithms. However, nonlinear weight update is unavoidable in any physical programmable resistors when
their whole dynamic ranges are exploited. Blind write methods are the most efficient learning schemes,
however, require even more ideal synaptic behaviors. The closed-loop read-write-verify method could
tolerate most non-ideal synaptic behaviors, however, it is expensive and should not be preferred for in situ
deep learning in a practical system. The periodical carry algorithm and its variations show great tolerance to
various non-idealities of synaptic behavior of memristive devices and request low complexity of peripheral
circuits around the crossbar array. These algorithms are underdeveloped and need further verifications in
real hardware systems.
In conclusion, we reviewed the necessary components for deep learning and their difficulties in being
implemented and accelerated by the crossbar array of analog programmable resistors. Only the information
forwarding process is well studied, whereas the error backpropagations and weight updates do not have a
clear path yet. We compare techniques that have been proposed to incorporate all the necessary components
to enable the LiM scheme, elucidating their drawbacks and merits when adapted to noisy and non-ideal
programmable resistors. We hope this work will bridge the gap between the device engineers and neural
network algorithmists.
