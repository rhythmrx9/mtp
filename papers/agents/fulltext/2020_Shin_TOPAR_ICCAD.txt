A Thermal-aware Optimization Framework for ReRAM-based
Deep Neural Network Acceleration
Hyein Shin, Myeonggu Kang, and Lee-Sup Kim

ABSTRACT
Resistive RAM (ReRAM) is widely regarded as a promising platform for deep neural network (DNN) acceleration. However, the
ReRAM device suffers from severe thermal problems that degrade
the lifetime and inference accuracy of the ReRAM-based DNN
accelerator. To address the issues, we propose a thermal-aware
optimization framework for accelerating DNN on ReRAM (TOPAR).
TOPAR includes 3-stage offline thermal optimization and online
thermal-aware error compensation. Offline thermal optimization
consists of thermal-aware weight decomposition, thermal-aware
column reordering, and fine-grained weight adjustment to reduce
the temperature of the ReRAM-based DNN accelerator. For online
thermal-aware error compensation, we compensate conductance
change according to the temperature variation. With TOPAR, the
endurance degradation due to temperature rise improves up to
2.39×, and inference accuracy is preserved without harming the
performance of the ReRAM-based DNN accelerator.

INTRODUCTION

Processing in-memory (PIM) approaches have been emerged to
address the memory wall problem of the conventional DNN accelerators. Among various platforms of PIM, ReRAM is widely regarded
as a proper platform for PIM with its intrinsic characteristic of
matrix-vector multiplication [2, 11, 18]. Owing to the current-sum
manner of ReRAM arrays, ReRAM-based DNN accelerators achieve
high performance and energy-efficient computation.
Despite the promising computation ability of ReRAM-based DNN
accelerators, thermal issues of ReRAM hinder its practical usage. According to [16], the endurance of the ReRAM cell reduces to 0.026×
as temperature increases from 300K to 380K. Also, the conductance
of ReRAM is extremely sensitive to temperature, which incurs more
than 90% inference accuracy degradation at high temperature [23].
Furthermore, according to our observation, the thermal problems
get more severe in ReRAM with low cell resolution (1∼4bit), which
is regarded as a realistic ReRAM compared to ideal ReRAM with
high cell resolution (7∼8bit) [2, 3, 11, 18]. Therefore, thermal problems of ReRAM should be mitigated as endurance and inference
accuracy are crucial factors for practical usage of the ReRAM-based
DNN accelerator.

[16, 17] aimed to enhance the endurance of the ReRAM cell by
reducing peak temperature. However, their target application was
not DNN acceleration, so the proposed schemes are not applicable
to ReRAM-based DNN accelerators properly. Meanwhile, [15, 23]
prevented accuracy degradation caused by temperature increase.
[15] proposed thermal-aware row adjustment which maps significant weights with large values to cold cells periodically, while [23]
mitigated accuracy degradation with bitwidth downgrading. However, [15] is not feasible with large scale DNNs, and its periodical
writing on ReRAM cell harms the endurance of cells. [23] proposed
method for large scale DNNs, but bitwidth downgrading of [23]
is not applicable to the practical ReRAM with low cell resolution
(1∼4bit). For the ReRAM with low cell resolution, severe accuracy
degradation occurs since it approximates LSB stored in the ReRAM
cell, and the error is amplified by shift and add (S&A) process. Furthermore, there have been no previous works optimizing endurance
and inference accuracy both.
In this work, we propose a thermal-aware optimization framework, TOPAR, which aims to both improve the endurance and
preserve the inference accuracy of the conventional ReRAM-based
DNN accelerator. TOPAR consists of offline and online thermal
optimization. We perform offline thermal optimization utilizing the
structure of the conventional ReRAM-based DNN accelerator. With
the individual weight-level and array-level thermal optimization,
our framework reduces average temperature and temperature variance between arrays for endurance enhancement in offline without
harming the performance of the accelerator. In online thermal optimization, we propose thermal-aware error compensation, which is
effective on the ReRAM with practical cell resolution and large scale
DNN models. The major contributions of this paper are summarized
as follows:
• We present TOPAR, an offline and online thermal-aware
optimization framework, to enhance endurance and prevent
inference accuracy degradation of the conventional ReRAMbased DNN accelerators.
• We propose 3-stage offline thermal optimization including
thermal-aware weight decomposition, thermal-aware column reordering, and fine-grained weight adjustment to reduce average temperature and temperature variance between
ReRAM arrays when running DNNs.
• We present online error compensation to preserve inference
accuracy regardless of variation on the conductance value
of the ReRAM cell.
• We evaluate TOPAR with five modern DNN models. Evaluation results show that TOPAR successfully enhances endurance and preserves the inference accuracy of ReRAMbased DNN accelerators.

2 BACKGROUND AND MOTIVATION
2.1 ReRAM-based DNN accelerator
Architecture of the conventional ReRAM-based DNN accelerator is
depicted in Fig. 1(a) [3, 18]. A heat sink, heat spreader, and thermal
interface material compose a cooling system for the accelerator [9].
ReRAM arrays on the silicon die perform matrix-vector multiplication. Fig. 1(b) shows an example of weight allocation on ReRAM
crossbar arrays. Since weights are signed values, a single weight is
represented with both positive and negative cells (𝑊 𝑝𝑜𝑠 and 𝑊 𝑛𝑒𝑔 )
as conductance values shown in Fig. 1(b). For the positive weight,
the absolute value of weight is stored in the positive array, and zero
value is stored in the corresponding negative array. For the negative
weight case, the absolute value of weight is stored in the negative
array, and zero value is stored in the corresponding positive array.
Also, each 𝑊 𝑝𝑜𝑠 and 𝑊 𝑛𝑒𝑔 is split into multiple ReRAM cells
as partial weights. As shown in Fig. 1(b), 8bit 𝑊 𝑝𝑜𝑠 and 𝑊 𝑛𝑒𝑔 are

𝑖 indicates 𝑖 𝑡ℎ partial weight. For matrix-vector multiplication between input features and weights, input features are applied to the
word line through multiple time steps depending on the resolution

endurance by reducing the peak temperature of ReRAM arrays, but

the application-specific characteristics of caching or search-based
PIM, respectively. Therefore, a novel method reducing average and
temperature variance for the ReRAM-based DNN accelerator is
required to enhance the lifetime of the accelerator.

THERMAL-AWARE OPTIMIZATION FOR
RERAM-BASED DNN ACCELERATION

We propose a thermal-aware optimization framework, TOPAR, to
address the thermal problems of ReRAM-based DNN accelerators.
As shown in Fig. 3, TOPAR consists of offline and online thermal
optimization. TOPAR takes the target DNN model and hardware
configurations, including cell resolution and array size as the inputs,
and performs 3-stage offline and online thermal optimization. In
the first stage of the offline optimization, 1 thermal-aware weight
decomposition is performed to reduce average temperature and
temperature variance at the individual weight-level. The next two
stages of offline thermal optimization, including thermal-aware
column reordering and fine-grained weight adjustment, focus on
reducing the temperature difference between arrays to enhance
endurance and preserve inference accuracy further. As they aim
to reduce the temperature difference between arrays, 2 thermalaware column reordering is performed based on a group of multiple
arrays. 3 Fine-grained weight adjustment is performed within a
pair of the positive and negative array for further alleviating the
temperature difference between positive and negative array. Consequently, the target DNN model is allocated to ReRAM arrays with
offline thermal optimization. While running the ReRAM-based DNN
accelerator with allocated weights, online thermal optimization restores distorted current-sum results with the 4 thermal-aware
error compensation to prevent inference accuracy degradation.

3.2

Thermal-aware column reordering

As stated in section 2.2, temperature between arrays shows large
difference. Therefore, array-level thermal optimization reducing the
temperature difference between arrays is required. In the ReRAMbased DNN accelerator, weights of DNNs are allocated into ReRAM
arrays as stated in [11]. Input channels are allocated into bit line
direction, and output channels are allocated into word line direction
across multiple ReRAM arrays as shown in Fig. 5(a). As each column
of ReRAM array corresponds to output channels of DNN, shuffling
the order of columns in ReRAM arrays changes the distribution of
weights allocated in each array without harming the computation
of DNN. Accordingly, we propose a thermal-aware column reordering, which reduces the temperature variance between arrays
by shuffling the order of columns in ReRAM arrays.
For reordering columns, the architecture of the ReRAM-based
DNN accelerator should be considered. 1 Positive and negative
array work as a pair for analog subtraction as described in section
2.1. Therefore, each column of the positive and negative array
should be reordered together in pairs. 2 A weight value is split into
multiple partial weights according to the cell resolution and mapped
into multiple columns. Since the partial weights are accumulated
via the S&A process, the partial weights should also be reordered
together. Therefore, we set the reordering pair as shown in Fig. 5(b).
Note that the order of rows remains unchanged so that the order
of channels in the input feature does not change.
To reduce the temperature difference between arrays by shuffling
the order of pairs, reordering criteria should take into account the
constraint that cells in positive and negative arrays within a pair
are reordered together, but they are allocated into different arrays.

To enlarge the effect of the column reordering, we reorder columns within a split input channel since the input channels are
already split according to the size of the ReRAM array as shown in
Fig. 5(a). As we reorder columns within a split input channel, the
order of current-sum results should be unshuffled to the original
order for obtaining input features for the next layer. However, huge
performance loss and hardware overhead occur for unshuffling
when we shuffle all pairs in the same split input channel. Therefore,
we set reordering range as four sets of positive and negative arrays
to avoid performance loss of the ReRAM-based DNN accelerator
while maintaining the effectiveness of column reordering. With the
reordering range, the additional 4:4 router routes current-sum result
to output registers to revert the original order without harming
the performance of the accelerator. Further analysis of hardware
overhead for unshuffling is explained in section 4.6.

3.3

Fine-grained weight adjustment

With the thermal-aware weight decomposition (section 3.1), there
are several decomposition cases having the same minimum cost.
For example, weight value 10 has the same minimum cost when it is
decomposed to either (10,0) or (16,6) as shown in Fig. 6(a). Although
the cost is the same regardless of which one is selected at the
individual weight-level, the two decomposition cases differ when
considering the structure of ReRAM-based DNN accelerators. As
weights are decomposed and stored into positive and negative array
separately, the temperature rise of the positive and negative array
varies depending on the selected decomposition case. With defining

4.6

Overhead analysis

Even though TOPAR aims to perform thermal optimization with
least hardware modification on the conventional ReRAM-based

DNN accelerator, thermal-aware column reordering and error compensation require hardware modification. The hardware overhead
for thermal-aware column reordering is modeled with a Verilog RTL,
synthesized using Synopsys Design Compiler under CMOS 65nm
library, and scaled to 32nm. The hardware overhead for thermalaware error compensation is estimated by post-layout simulation
under CMOS 65nm process, and also scaled to 32nm. In the case
of thermal-optimized column reordering, an additional 4:4 router
and registers for storing the original order of columns are required,
and performance loss does not exist since we limit the reordering
range. For the additional blocks, area and power overhead account
for 0.012% and 0.021% of total ReRAM-based DNN accelerator, respectively. In the case of current mirror-based error compensation,
area overhead is estimated to be 0.8% and power overhead is less
than 0.001% of total ReRAM-based DNN accelerator. Consequently,
the hardware modification required for TOPAR is negligibly small.

5

CONCLUSION

In this paper, we proposed the thermal-aware optimization framework for ReRAM-based DNN accelerators. We presented 3-stage
offline thermal optimization and online error compensation to address the thermal issues of the ReRAM device. With our thermal
optimization, average temperature and temperature variance of
arrays decreased up to 7.4K and 52.1%, respectively. Owing to the
temperature reduction, we improved the lifetime of the accelerator
up to 2.39×. Also, inference accuracy had been preserved by our
thermal-aware error compensation with less than 1% accuracy loss.
