Transformer-based Large Language Models (LLMs) have made signiicant progress in the ields of computer vision and
natural language processing. The use of LLMs in embedded and real-time systems to provide more powerful intelligence
capabilities is also an inevitable trend. However, the traditional von Neumann architecture encounters great challenges with
transformer-based LLMs due to huge data movement overheads. Processing-in-Memory (PIM) emerges as a great potential
solution by signiicantly closing the gap between computation and storage. Nevertheless, transformer-based LLMs usually
consist of multiple transformer blocks, and the operations within each transformer block vary signiicantly on computation
and memory access characteristics. Existing PIM designs that rely solely on SRAM, DRAM, or ReRAM cannot adapt well to
transformer-based LLMs. To address these challenges, we introduce HyPIM, a 3D hybrid PIM architecture that integrates SRAM
and ReRAM slices to process through their cooperation. However, HyPIM does not change the scenario where the inference
latency of the attention mechanism continues to dominate the overall inference latency in the transformer-based LLMs
when dealing with long sequences of data. Exploiting these insights, we then design a hardware-friendly early termination
approximation algorithm and a sparse matrix multiplication strategy for the HyPIM architecture, reducing the overhead
through a software and hardware co-design. Compared with the state-of-the-art Newton, TransPIM, HAIMA, HARDSEA, and
H3DAtten architectures, the proposed HyPIM architecture achieves up to a 1.27×ś1.67× latency improvement.

intelligent, eicient, and responsive, thereby serving a wide range of practical application scenarios. Furthermore,

However, the performance of transformer-based LLMs often beneits from a massive growth in the number of
model parameters and computational costs. In traditional von Neumann architectures, the processor and memory
are separated. The transformer’s frequent matrix multiplication operations necessitate constant data transfers
between these two components, resulting in considerable latency. The data movement time of transformer-based
LLMs on the traditional von Neumann architecture exceeds 60% [54]. Thus, data movement has emerged as a
non-negligible bottleneck of transformer-based LLMs.
Processing-in-Memory (PIM) architectures [2] enable computation to be performed directly within memory,
thereby signiicantly reducing data-movement overhead, and have been regarded as a promising approach to
accelerate transformer-based LLMs. For example, TransPIM [54] using bit-serial row parallel PIM operations
substantially reduce data-movement overhead, which highlights the potential of PIM for LLM acceleration.
To date, PIM architectures have been widely used to accelerate convolutional neural networks (CNNs). Compared with CNNs that exhibit relatively regular computation patterns, transformer-based models show pronounced
heterogeneity across modules in both computation and memory-access characteristics. Therefore, although recent
eforts have begun to explore PIM-based accelerators [54] for transformer-based models, such module-level
disparities make it diicult for existing PIM designs to execute all LLM modules eiciently.
Firstly, current architectures often overlook the variability of access and computational characteristics among
transformer modules, which means they cannot adapt well to PIM architectures based on sole hardware design.
Dynamic Random Access Memory (DRAM), Static Random Access Memory (SRAM) , and Resistive Random
Access Memory (ReRAM) are all hot topics in the research of PIM architecture. However, they difer in structure,
operational principle and performance, so PIM architectures based on diferent hardware designs are often used
to handle diferent types of tasks. For example, area eiciency of SRAM is low, but read/write eiciency and
durability are very high, so SRAM-based architectures are more suitable for dealing with small-scale applications
that require fast and frequent access, such as dot product in the attention module. Area and read/write eiciency
of ReRAM is high, but its endurance is lower. Thus, ReRAM-based architectures are more suitable for applications
that require high-capacity storage and a certain level of eiciency, such as linear layers in the transformer.
Therefore, the eicient processing of the complex structure of transformer-based LLMs requires the collaboration
of multiple hardware PIMs.
Secondly, the traditional interconnection of multiple hardware is a separated connection architecture based on
the external bus and interface, which leads to signiicant penalties in latency, bandwidth, and storage eiciency.
Simultaneously, the increase of chip computing power requires more computing units, which leads to signiicant
losses in energy and area eiciency [23]. Therefore, PIM architectures requires more eicient on-chip network
and higher storage density.
Finally, most existing PIM architectures are aimed at computation-intensive tasks, such as CNNs, which use
all resources to process the tasks of one layer at a time. This strategy leads to large and frequent data transfers
when processing memory-intensive tasks. Therefore, it is necessary to plan the task execution low and datalow
of LLMs reasonably. Additionally, the computational complexity of the attention mechanism is proportional to
the square of the sequence length. As the sequence length increases, the latency of attention will dominate the
overall latency. Unfortunately, the attention optimization strategy used in traditional von Neumann architectures
is not applicable in PIM architectures due to the complex processing and mapping of data.

Based on the above discussion, we introduce a Hybrid 3D PIM architecture (HyPIM) that improves parallel
eiciency and decreases the inference latency of transformer-based LLMs. To address variations in resource

• A hybrid 3D PIM architecture, called HyPIM, is proposed. Stacked ReRAM-based memory slices are utilized
for tasks that demand extensive storage capacity, including linear operations. Meanwhile, stacked SRAMbased memory slices are employed to execute the dot product and matrix multiplication operations in the
attention module, which need high eiciency.
• A mixed-granularity parallel acceleration strategy for transformers is designed. The execution order and
datalow pipeline of the transformer modules in the architecture is planned rationally.
• An attention acceleration strategy is implemented on the HyPIM architecture. The use of the computational
properties of softmax allows for the early termination of computations that have no impact on the results.

Motivation for Accelerate Transformer

Frequent matrix multiplications during the inference process of transformers result in increased data movement
costs, which dominate the inference latency [54]. Thus, PIM architectures minimizing data movement have
become a popular approach for accelerating neural networks. While PIM architectures for CNNs have been
extensively researched, they do not directly apply to transformers. The following challenges exist:
Challenge 1: The singular storage hardware PIM architecture is unsuitable for the structure of the transformer
model.
Existing PIM architectures are often built on a single storage hardware [19, 44]. This design fails to consider the
varying storage and computational resource demands of diferent network modules, resulting in poor resource
utilization and compute eiciency. As is shown in Table 1, the characteristics of memory device technologies
difer signiicantly [25].
The weights of the pre-trained model remain unchanged during the inference process, and they can be preloaded into memory to avoid repeated read/write operations. However, this approach consumes a signiicant
amount of memory resources. In contrast, all data of the attention mechanism are generated and loaded into the
computational array during the inference, thus requiring high read/write operation frequency and eiciency.
The computational delay caused by the mismatch between task and hardware characteristics can signiicantly
impact the overall inference delay. Therefore, compared with the use of DRAM-PIM and traditional accelerators
to accelerate transformer inference [29, 38], hybrid PIM architectures based on ReRAM with high memory density
and SRAM with high read/write eiciency are ideal for accelerating complex transformers.
Challenge 2: The traditional connection between the two types of hardware incurs signiicant penalties in bandwidth,
storage, and computing eiciency.
The traditional connection mode between hardware usually involves using external buses or interface data
transfer channels to connect diferent hardware supporting PIM chips and processors responsible for processing
tasks. This segregated architecture incurs severe losses in bandwidth, storage, and computational eiciency,
especially when dealing with large-scale data or real-time applications. In the traditional 2D architecture, multiple
diferent storage devices and processors are usually located in diferent packages, which leads to a waste of
storage space and area, and increases the data transmission path, thus afecting the computing eiciency [23, 34].
To overcome these limitations, a hybrid 3D PIM architecture based on 3D memory structure is proposed.
The architecture stacks multiple ReRAM and SRAM dies through M3D integration technology to eliminate the
inherent limitations of the traditional connection mode. In this way, the physical transmission distance between
the storage unit and the computing unit is greatly shortened, and the data exchange delay between the slices is
signiicantly reduced. At the same time, due to the increase of vertical interconnection density, the bandwidth
is signiicantly enhanced, which can not only improve energy eiciency during cross-layer data transmission,

Challenge 3: Currently available attention optimizations are diicult to map eiciently onto PIM architecture.
The attention mechanism calculates similarity among all elements with a time complexity of � (� 2 ). As

mechanism, resulting in signiicant performance costs for the model. In another sense, the model’s size is
restricted.
Various optimization algorithms have been designed for attention mechanisms, including techniques such as
pruning, quantization, and approximate computation. Quantization and approximate computing usually rely on
general-purpose computing units to perform complex format conversion and mapping of data. Then, some works
also use the cache and memory access optimization in the von Neumann architecture to improve computational
eiciency. However, in PIM architecture, the computing unit is limited to the native operations of the memory
array, and its computation and storage are highly coupled, which makes it diicult to eiciently support complex
indexing, data rearrangement and dynamic control low. These optimization methods are not only diicult to map
on PIM, but also may introduce additional data movement and energy consumption overhead, which weakens
the energy eiciency advantage of PIM.
In this paper, we focus on the two primary computations that have the most signiicant efect on the attention
mechanism: the S dot product calculation and the A matrix calculation. Combining their diferent computing
characteristics and hardware features, a software and hardware collaborative strategy suitable for the PIM
architecture was designed to improve eiciency.

3 HyPIM overview
HyPIM is a PIM accelerator speciically designed for transformer inference. Its architecture is built on M3D
Integrated Circuit (IC), a currently promising 3D IC technology [4, 45, 49] that stacks multiple functional layers
capable of storing and computing. The highly integrated and heterogeneous architecture is compatible with
CPUs, GPUs, and other devices. This section provides a comprehensive overview of the datalow, fundamental
components and parallel strategy of HyPIM. Subsequent sections introduce the optimization strategies for the
attention mechanism.

Infrastructure of HyPIM

Fig. 4. Overview of HyPIM Architecture

Figure 4 provides an overview of the HyPIM architecture, which supports the execution low described in the

Matrix-Vector Multiplication Unit (MVMU). The MVMUs of HyPIM are classiied into two types: the
ReRAM-based MVM unit (R-MVMU); and the SRAM-based MVM unit (S-MVMU). R-MVMU has the capability
to pre-load the weight data into the computational array prior to inference, unlike S-MVMU which needs to
gradually load the data into the computational array during the inference process. The array’s original size is set

to 256×256, which is well suited for long-sequence workloads. All rows share the input bufer of each MVMU,
and all columns share the output bufer. In each cycle, the controller of the MVMU will read the input data
required for each row from the input bufer and load them into the input registers of the corresponding rows.
The controller will records the indices to route partial sums to the corresponding accumulation. The MVMU
then converts the digital signals into analog voltages applied to each row of the MVMU via DACs and the array
starts performing analog matrix multiplication operations. The diference is that the output data of the R-MVMU
is represented as the accumulated current value, while the output data of the S-MVMU is represented as the
accumulated voltage diference between bitline (BL) and bitline bar (BLB). Eventually, they are both converted
back to digital signal values via ADCs for transmission to the next module.
Accumulate Queue. The MVMU arrays in this architecture only store 1-bit values per cell, and the n-bits
precision values are assigned to diferent arrays to complete the calculation, and the calculation results of each
array go through the S&A unit to obtain the actual data and accumulate to obtain the inal n-bits calculation
results. The data obtained by the MVMU are placed in the corresponding accumulation queue according to the
corresponding index. In R-MVMU, all data are processed in parallel and the resulting computations are saved to
the corresponding accumulation queue, completing partial sum accumulative processes. In contrast, S-MVMU
utilizes a time-domain SIMD-based design due to resource limitations. This design can be selected to execute
multiple MVM operations serially in multiple cycles. The results of each computation cycle are stored in the
accumulation queue, based on their indices, to complete the overall accumulation process. Once the accumulation
queue connected to a speciic index has completed all the accumulations, the data within the queue are extracted
and deposited into the output bufer.
Nonlinear and Functional Units. Nonlinear calculations are performed through lookup tables, stored in a
ReRAM slice with high storage density, providing a means of high accuracy computation. To reduce the size of
the nonlinear calculation lookup table, we split the �-bits data into high and low �2 -bits. This method allows us
to lookup data in the table based on the high and low �2 -bits, and integrate them to obtain the inal result. The
functional unit mainly includes the division unit and the addition unit. The division unit is mainly on SRAM slice.
After the element of S ′ is calculated by dot product, the corresponding element is directly obtained through the
division unit, so that the corresponding element of S can be quickly found in the lookup table. The addition unit
adds the input elements and output elements of the attention module or FFN module after the output elements
are obtained, so that the next normalization process can be completed quickly.
Communication Method. The functional layer employs a communication method based on M3D-based
3D small-world network-on-chip [8] (M3D-SW-NoC) which is designed with a topology that possesses a short
average path length and high global connectivity. Improved local connectivity of nodes is achieved with the
addition of random short-distance connections while maintaining global connectivity between further nodes.
This design results in shorter communication paths between nodes, thereby reducing communication delays
and enhancing local communication eiciency. The intra-layer communication technique combines a 2D mesh
topology with a tree topology. The single chip array is composed of several uniformly situated tiles, which are
connected via a 2D mesh communication network as a component of the M3D-SW-NoC. The tree topology is
implemented within the tile to link each S-MVMU/R-MVMU arrays and other functional modules.

As shown in Figure 5(a), in the calculation process, the calculation of Q, K, and V can be started in parallel on
ReRAM-PIM, and the results are transmitted to SRAM-PIM through high-bandwidth inter-layer interconnection.
Among them, the calculation result of Q, K will be used as the input of S, so the calculation of S must be started
after the calculation of Q, K is completed. Then, the calculation results of V and S are jointly used to generate A,
so the start time of the calculation of A needs to be later than the completion time of the calculation of S and V.
Both S and A are computed on SRAM-PIM, where Softmax is implemented using lookup tables (LUTs) stored in
ReRAM. Subsequently, the result of A will be transmitted to ReRAM-PIM for subsequent computation. Similarly,
the computation of the FFN depends on the output of A, so its start time must be later than the completion time
of A’s computation.
Most traditional architectures execute only a single network layer at a time, which means that all compute
units are used only for that layer, thus achieving signiicant parallelism. However, this is not applicable in PIM
architectures with high storage density and may lead to a considerable waste of communication and storage
resources. Therefore, it is imperative to develop an appropriate ine-grained pipeline for PIM architectures. If the
computation of the previous layer generates enough output data to support the computation of part of the array
in the later layer, our architecture will forward this data to the array containing the parameters of the next layer
through the NoC, thus starting the computation of the subsequent layers ahead.
In Figure 5(b), the weight matrix of size 512×512 is divided into four parts, which are stored in four MVMUs.
As shown in part (a), in step1, when a subvector of input vector 1 arrives, it can perform basic operations with

obtain the complete output. As shown in part (b), when a complete input vector is ready, the weight matrix will
participate in the calculation at the same time to obtain the complete result of the output vector. In step 1, the irst

4 Atention mechanism synergy optimisation strategy
The attention module is a crucial component of the transformer model. When dealing with a long sequence of
data, its computational characteristics result in the attention module taking up the majority of the computational
and latency costs of the overall inference process. This makes the attention module a performance bottleneck for
transformer-based LLM inference. Hence, optimizing and accelerating the attention component is imperative.
However, current approaches for expediting the attention module are often inapplicable in PIM architectures.
The existing attention optimization on PIM mostly adopts static threshold or top-K strategy [34], which often
brings accuracy loss while reducing redundant calculations. Our proposed dynamic threshold mechanism can
adaptively retain critical non-zero values and maintain higher accuracy while reducing redundancy. In addition,
although HARDSEA [34] and other methods can skip zero values, the array utilization is low. Our hardwaresoftware collaboration strategy can balance the eiciency and resource utilization in sparse computing. This
chapter seeks to examine acceleration techniques for the attention mechanism in PIM architecture, with the
objective of achieving more efective attention acceleration. We focus on two core computational operations in
the attention mechanism.

4.1

Early Calculation Termination Strategy

The irst core operation of the attention module is S ′ = QK ⊤ , which involves performing dense matrix multiplication whose outputs become efectively sparse after softmax. The attention module employs softmax to
selectively attend to elements in the input sequence that hold relevant information by assigning higher attention
weight to them. This method accentuates the disparities between a restricted number of large elements and the
majority of smaller ones, consequently producing a substantial quantity of negligible data that does not inluence
the outcome. If we can establish that the data resulting from the primary calculations is small enough, we can
halt the calculations prematurely.
The �3 [15] approximation algorithm is also an early termination algorithm based on this observation. The
algorithm sorts the elements of each column in the K matrix in the preprocessing process, so that the results
obtained in the calculation process can gradually converge to the inal calculation result. When the diference
between the partial sum of a stage and the maximum value is too large, it can be considered that the diference

Unfortunately, in PIM operations that require a high degree of parallelism, it may lead to repeated loading of

Each unit of SRAM-PIM analog circuit array only supports 1 bit of data, so under the limited computing

Co-Optimization Strategy for Sparse Matrix Multiplication

The second core operation of the attention module is A = SV, which involves sparse matrix multiplication
after the above strategy. Speciically, the large number of zero elements in the sparse matrix leads to waste of
computational resources and increased inference latency. Most existing work stores and processes sparse data
using formats such as COO or CSR. However, the hardware architectures of existing accelerators (e.g., GPUs) are
ineicient for sparse operations (e.g., SpMV/SpMM). Hardware devices based on systolic array architecture, such
as TPU, do not support to skip zero calculation due to the strict internal connectivity. PIM architecture has rigid
interconnection similar to systolic array, and the processing of sparse matrix multiplication will lead to a lot of
waste of resources.
However, in the calculation of SRAM-PIM, the dense matrix V of the module is pre-loaded into the SRAM
array as a weight. Since the calculation process of each score vector in the score matrix basically does not afect
each other, each array is actually responsible for the calculation of the V matrix and the sparse row vector in the
matrix S. Therefore, the calculation of this part of the attention mechanism can be abstracted into the calculation
between multiple sparse vectors and dense matrix. Each row of compute units in the array is responsible for the
calculation of a single input element with multiple weights stored in that row. Therefore, in the case of no complex

been proposed to accelerate CNN inference and training by exploiting the sparsity of weights or activations and the
high parallelism in execution. SCNN [40] achieves model compression and acceleration by reducing unnecessary
connections and computations. SnapeA [1] designs a predictable early activation strategy to determine part of

Transformer Accelerator: Due to the high calculation proportion of attention mechanism in the execution

the attention mechanism. Dota [42] ilters the weights by generating a mask matrix of score weights through
low-rank transformation of the matrix, and designs an end-to-end inference acceleration by using the parallelism
between tokens. Sanger [37] focuses on the attention score calculation and attention output calculation of the
attention mechanism at the same time, and uses the quantitative prediction based on the attention matrix to
dynamically sparse attention. Some works [13] focus on both the FFN and attention modules and optimizes them
jointly. Our paper chooses to focus on both the attention score calculation and the attention output calculation of
the attention mechanism simultaneously and implements transformer acceleration through the software and
hardware co-design of the early termination and sparse processing strategy.
PIM Accelerator. In order to reduce the cost of data movement during task execution, various PIM accelerators
have been proposed. Newton [17], and TransPIM [54] are digital PIM accelerators designed based on DRAM.
They adopt near-memory architecture which places the computing units in DRAM bank to reduce overhead
by high-speed in-memory bandwidth in HBM. In contrast, TWT [47] , Colonnade [24] are SRAM-based CIM
architectures [27, 52], in which part of the work adopts digital CIM arrays to integrate the computing cells
near the storage cells. In the other part of the work, the analog PIM array is used to complete the analog signal
calculation in the memory cell by using the characteristics of the SRAM cell. ReRAM, a new non-volatile memory
technology, comes into the ield of PIM design because of its high storage density and high eiciency [46].
Some PIM architectures such as ISAAC [43], W2W-PIM [31], PRIME [6], and RENO [35] use ReRAM crossbar
memory arrays to perform analog data computations. Based on this, the existing research has explored many
aspects of large model acceleration under the framework of PIM. Accelerators based on 3D stacked DRAM
[44] provide the large capacity required for large models. PipePIM [19] improves the utilization of DRAMbased PIM architecture with pipeline and double bufering. TransPIM [54] adopts a tokens-based data low and
makes lightweight modiications in the conventional High-Bandwidth Memory (HBM) architecture to support
transformer’s hybrid PIM/NMC processing and eicient data communication. However, they do not fully consider
the characteristics of diferent modules, and are still limited to a single hardware PIM architecture. By taking
into account the characteristics of diferent computations in transformers [21], HAIMA [10] proposes hybrid
PIM architectures that exploit the cooperation between SRAM and DRAM to accelerate diferent operations.
SpecPIM [29] uses heterogeneous PIM architecture to accelerate speculative reasoning, and integrates PIM
engine in GDDR6. H3d-transformer [38] uses 3D technology to heterogeneous DRAM-PIM and TPU for diferent
module characteristics of Transformer. H3DAtten [30] proposed the heterogeneous 3D-PIM architecture of
ReRAM+SRAM and considered the hardware characteristics. However, the above works basically do not consider
the combination of PIM architecture to optimize the attention module with large computation in the transformer
model. HARDSEA [34] also uses ReRAM+SRAM heterogeneous architecture and optimizes attention processing,
but lacks 3D stacking support and is limited in area eiciency. In this paper, Heterogeneous 3D reram and sram
architecture is used to accelerate diferent operations of transformer. At the same time, software and hardware
co-optimization is done for the attention module with a large calculation, so as to realize the double acceleration
of data movement and computation cost.

7 Conclusion
In this paper, we propose HyPIM, a 3D hybrid memory PIM architecture that employs optimization strategies
for transformers and attention modules to accelerate LLMs. The architecture leverages high-eiciency SRAM
storage and high-capacity ReRAM storage co-processing to address the imbalance between storage and computing
resources that both traditional architectures and conventional PIM architectures face when processing transformer
modules. Additionally, we recognize the importance of the attention module in transformer-based LLM reasoning
and reduce its impact on overall reasoning delay through a software and hardware co-design of an early
termination algorithm and a sparse data processing algorithm for the two core calculations of the attention
module. Our architecture achieves up to a 1.27×ś1.67× latency improvement over other state-of-the-art PIM
architectures, such as Newton, TransPIM, HAIMA, HARDSEA, and H3DAtten, while also demonstrating strong
robustness and scalability, thereby opening up opportunities for further advances in LLM development.
Our work focuses on real-time LLM workloads, speciically large model applications used in embedded and
real-time systems. Additionally, our approach accelerates LLMs with limited resources, improves the real-time
response speed of large model applications like LLMs, and eiciently executes applications such as embedded
end-side LLMs. At the same time, it is compatible with existing mainstream processors, making it easy to integrate
into various embedded platforms.
Accelerating LLMs through software and hardware collaboration holds great promise for the future of embedded
real-time workloads. By leveraging hardware accelerators and advanced hardware-software combined algorithms,
embedded devices can perform complex language model inference with low power consumption, low latency,
and high eiciency. This provides strong support for application scenarios such as edge computing, real-time
response, and multitasking. It will accelerate technological innovation in ields such as autonomous driving and
intelligent healthcare, and promote the widespread adoption of intelligent embedded systems.
There is still signiicant room for improvement in our work. By further customizing the design of hardware accelerators and adapting LLMs, as well as optimizing intelligent algorithms to align with architectural
characteristics, we can achieve greater eiciency and stronger scalability.
In short, this architecture provides an eicient and economical solution for LLM real-time workloads, which
can be further integrated with embedded real-time systems to serve various practical application scenarios more
intelligently, eiciently, and quickly.
