Abstract—PoolFormer is a type of neural network architecture
that is abstracted from Transformer where the computationally
heavy token mixer module is replaced with simple pooling
function. This paper presents a memristor-based PoolFormer
modeling and training framework for edge-AI applications. To fit
for implementation on resistive crossbar array, original
PoolFormer structure is further optimized by replacing
normalization operation with hardware friendly scaling operation.
In addition, the non-idealities of RRAM crossbar from device to
array level as well as peripheral readout circuits are also included.
By incorporating these elements under a single framework for
network training , their impact to the network performance can
be effectively mitigated. This framework is implemented in a
combination of Python and PyTorch. A 16-block PoolFormer
network is designed and optimized for CIFAR-10 image
classification tasks using measured 64×64 RRAM crossbar array
results. The total network weights are only 0.26M, which is at least
one order of magnitude smaller than that of the conventional DNN
implementation. When compared to the ideal model with FP64
weight bit-length, 85.86% inference accuracy is reached with only
4-level weight resolutions and less than 4% accuracy loss.
Keywords—Memristor crossbar, Transformer, PoolFormer,
Edge-AI, neuromorphic system

I. INTRODUCTION
Transformer has demonstrated remarkable classification
performance improvement in computer vision [1-6] and natural
language processing [7-8]. The superior performance of
Transformer is dependent on a lot of computing power required
by the self-attention operation. Although customized
Transformer accelerator chip [9-11] and cloud cluster [12] have
been reported in literature, these solutions primarily aim for
large-scale neural network applications. They are not suitable
for lightweight neural network running on battery powered
edge-AI devices which have limited storage memory,
computation power and battery capacity. Recent studies
unveiled that the competence of transformer model is largely
attributed to its general architecture, rather than the selfattention mechanism itself. In [13], a new model called
This work was supported by the Agency for Science, Technology and
research (A*STAR), Singapore under the Nanosystems at the Edge
programme, grant no. A18A1b0055.

PoolFormer is proposed by replacing the computationally heavy
self-attention operation with simple pooling operation.
Significant operation simplification is achieved with mild
compromise in performance. Since it requires no parameters in
the memory and the processing procedure is much simplified,
tremendous amount of computation resources can be saved.
On the other hand, resistive memristor crossbar arrays have
gained popularity recently because of their attractive features of
high data throughput and excellent energy efficiency.
Memristive devices have the unique ability to store weights due
to their non-volatile programmable conductance. Additionally,
by applying input voltage pulses and measuring the resulting
output current, the vector-matrix multiplication (VMM) process
can be performed in parallel, which results in a considerable
acceleration of overall network computing [14-16]. Therefore,
resistive crossbar array-based processing-in-memory (PIM)
architecture is an elegant solution to enhance both computation
speed and energy efficiency by several orders compared to
conventional memory [17-18].
The implementations of Transformer structure on memristor
crossbar array have been reported in literature [23]. There are
also efforts to build hardware-software co-design framework
with full circuit models to simulate and evaluate the
performance of Transfromer network in circuit implementation.
For example, the self-attention operation is decomposed to
reduce the frequency of intermediate results reloading and
transfers the Softmax function to logical inference with lookup
table [24]. However, these reported solutions still require
considerable computation resources.
Inspired by the success of PoolFormer in computer vision,
we aim to exploit its application to edge-AI and the hardware
implementation on memristive crossbar array in this work. A
modeling and training framework called RRAM-PoolFormer is
proposed. The key innovations in this work can be summarized
as follows. (1) Proposed a hardware friendly PoolFormer
structure suitable for implementation on memristor crossbar.
Specifically, self-attention operation is replaced with pooling as
an extremely weak token mixer. (2) Non-ideality of memristor
from device to array and readout circuits are incorporated into
network training process to effectively compensate PoolFormer

II. RRAM-POOLFORMER DESIGN AND IMPLEMENTATION
A. RRAM-PoolFormer Structure
The structure of the overall RRAM-PoolFormer network is
shown in Fig. 1(a). The RRAM block architecture is given in
Fig. 1(b). Compared to the conventional Transformer, the
proposed structure has following novelties to adapt for edge
device applications:
(1) To balance the performance of network and the circuit
efficiency, layer normalization operation is replaced by channel
scaling. The scaling factor for each channel is optimized
automaticaly during the network training process and then
applied to the inference tasks. Compared to the conventional
normalization operation, channel scaling can maintain
satisfactory accuracy on small-size datasets for edge-AI
applications while the computation requirement can be much
relaxed.
(2) Standard PoolFormer involves 4 stages, each stage has 𝐿⁄6,
𝐿⁄6, 𝐿⁄2 and 𝐿⁄6 blocks to process the data, where 𝐿 is the
total number of blocks. Since edge device applications focus

more on small datasets, requiring less storage and computation
resources. Hence, 2-stage structure with 12 and 4 blocks is
selected in the proposed RRAM-PoolFormer. Moreover, the
embedding dimension is chosen to be 32 and 64 for each stage
to reduce the total number of parameters. The final optmized
model has 2 stages with 16 × 16 and 8× 8 tokens.

III. IMPACT OF CROSSBAR ARRAY NONIDEALITIES
A. Device Non-idealities
Due to manufacturing imperfections, non-ideal memristor
device characteristics such conductance variation, nonlinearity
and programming failure are inevitable, and they can bring
significant impact to the network performance [32]. Therefore,
the non-idealities should be considered during the network
design stage and compensated appropriately. In each training
cycle, a random mask matrix that represents programming
failure is generated based on the recorded programming failure
rate, to simulate its impact. The array conductance matrix and
this programming failure mask are combined using AND
operation to get the programmed conductance value. It should
be noted that HRS status is assigned to the failed devices.
B. Crossbar Non-idealities
Sneak path and line resistance are the major contributors to
crossbar array non-idealities [33]. Because of the sneak
pathways and cumulative IR-drop on the interconnect traces,
there will be difference in node voltage between the theoretical
and actual cases. Conductance overlapping and line resistance
will combine, as seen in Fig. 4, to obscure the true conductance
levels and reduce the accuracy.

Iterative and linear techniques have been used in several
similar research to predict IR-drop in crossbar [34-35].
Applying the Kirchhoff's laws equations to calculate the
crossbar current distribution is the usual solution. Iterative
matrix algorithms can lower the time complexity to O(m2n2).
Even so, it still takes a lot of time, especially as the crossbar
size grows up. A novel strategy was put forth to handle the
voltage and current degradations as scaling factor matrices that
are applied to the output current and input voltage in order to
improve simulation speed. This model successfully combined
quick computation with O(mn) time complexity and low current
distortion is obtained. Further technical information is
explained in [36].
IV. SIMULATION RESULTS
The memristor model is built with the measured TaOx 2T1R
RRAM crossbar results [37]. 4-level device resistance in the
range of 5 kΩ to 30 kΩ is used. After the weight mapping to
crossbar array, the non-idealities-aware training is carried out
[31]. It took 2.7 hours to be trained on a 16-core 3.2GHz AMD
Ryzen 5800H CPU with 32GB RAM. For inference operation,
the effects of all non-idealities are assessed holistically and the
inference accuracy is shown in Fig. 5. With 64×64 array size,
0.5Ω line resistance and 15% device variation, the inference
accuracy reaches 85.86% within 500 epochs.
Fig. 6 illustrates how the device resolution affects training
accuracy. After mapping the weights to 2-8 bit resolutions, the
proposed training method with 2-bit weight only loses 3% of
inference accuracy compared to ideal software result. However,
conventional offline training has much higher accuracy
degradation. Fig. 7 displays the effect of line resistance on
inference accuracy. As the line resistance increases from 0 Ω to
2 Ω, the accuracy of the conventional training approach
experiences a substantial drop due to the change in voltage
distribution brought about by the line resistance. On the other
hand, the non-ideality aware training method experiences a
slight decrease in accuracy of less than 1%, still retaining similar
accuracy.

V. CONCLUSION
In this study, a PoolFormer modeling and training
framework for edge AI applications using memristors has been
presented. Furthermore, a framework that takes into account
non-idealities for software-hardware co-design of edge devices
based on memristive crossbars is described. The 16-layer
RRAM-PoolFormer with nonidealities achieved 85.86%
inference accuracy on the CIFAR-10 dataset while consuming
fewer resources compared to conventional deep neural network
implementations. This proposed technique offers a
comprehensive circuit solution to assess the performance of the
RRAM-PoolFormer model and determine the requirement for
low-resolution memristor crossbars for edge AI vision
applications.
