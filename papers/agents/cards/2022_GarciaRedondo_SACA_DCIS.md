# SACA

**SACA: System-level Analog CIM Accelerators Simulation Framework: Accurate Simulation of Non-Ideal Components** — Conference on Design of Circuits and Integrated Systems (DCIS 2022) (2022)

> ⚠ Analysis based on the abstract only (no full text was available). Treat claims/numbers below as unverified.

## TL;DR
SACA is a system-level simulation framework for host+CIM-accelerator TinyML systems that models device- and periphery-level non-idealities (including IR-drop) and reports accuracy/energy/performance trade-offs for a CIFAR-10 CNN and a Human Activity Recognition MLP.

## Summary
Always-on TinyML edge accelerators based on non-volatile-memory compute-in-memory (CIM) crossbars are constrained by noise and non-idealities that compromise DNN accuracy. The authors present SACA, a system-level framework that simulates a full host+CIM-accelerator system while accounting for device-level non-idealities and periphery (ADC/DAC and interconnect) characteristics, including IR-drop effects that become severe at larger crossbar sizes. The framework sweeps NVM technology choice, crossbar size, and periphery design to produce energy/performance/accuracy trade-off curves. It is demonstrated on two representative TinyML workloads: a CNN for CIFAR-10 image classification and a fully-connected network for Human Activity Recognition (HAR). The paper emphasizes that IR-drop on larger crossbars substantially degrades accuracy and system reliability, informing crossbar-size and technology choices for edge CIM designs.

## Contributions
- A system-level (host + CIM accelerator) simulation framework, SACA, targeting TinyML edge workloads
- Joint modeling of device-level non-idealities and periphery/circuit-level effects (including IR-drop)
- Exploration across NVM technology, crossbar size and periphery configuration to generate energy/performance/accuracy design curves
- Case studies on a CIFAR-10 CNN and a Human Activity Recognition fully-connected network
